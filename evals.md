# Evals

## Reading benchmark numbers
- **The harness decides the score**: the same benchmark measured ~40% via one CLI agent vs ~50% via a controlled harness, and ~20% vs ~40% for another. Cross-model comparisons need one controlled environment or the numbers are meaningless (see harnesses.md § What the harness is for the 22% SWE-Bench-Pro swing) [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Sharper framing: a single benchmark number under a single scaffold is **one sample from a distribution nobody measured** — "like using the screwdriver bit of a Swiss army knife to cut cheese and concluding the knife is broken". Cross-harness differencing is the primary cause of benchmark divergence, with high false-positive AND false-negative rates in SWE-shaped benchmarks invisible in headline stats [State of Data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyIoTOAbRfs) (2026-07)
- Inference providers are "throughput maxing, accuracy minimizing": across providers serving the *same* open-weight model a ~10-point spread was measured (76.4% vs 62.4%). Check your provider's accuracy before adopting — open weights get blamed for what the provider did [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- **Benchmark bugs are mundane and huge**: a maths benchmark correction moved a frontier model from ~50% to ~80%, caused by answer-extraction errors (dropped minus signs, off-by-ones). Fixing extraction alone raised one open model from 10% to 25%, and tokenization choices shift MMLU ~0.4%. Low open-weight scores are often formatting, not capability [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- **"Don't let the test writer grade the task."** The dominant contrived-data recipe: hire domain experts, have them chat-generate plausible tasks, solve them, cherry-pick the cases where the model diverged, package as a hard north-star benchmark — then sell the training data that hill-climbs that same benchmark. Goodhart with a profit motive; stop outsourcing your definition of realism to the vendors you buy evals from [State of Data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyIoTOAbRfs) (2026-07)
- **Counting right answers assumes every question is equally important.** Item response theory instead fits per-item difficulty and discrimination and estimates model ability on a shared normal scale, giving a likelihood interval rather than a bare percentage. On real epoch.ai data Opus 4.1 scored 245/337 and Gemini 3 Pro 247/337 — a tie by raw count, but ~1 SD apart in IRT ability, because the harder items are what separate them [Vidal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3FEoMYvUf8) (2026-07), method corroborated by [arXiv 2505.15055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15055)
- **Time-splits are the only real benchmark decontamination**: released questions and solutions leak into next-gen pretraining — collect only fresh tasks from the previous month and re-evaluate monthly [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06)
- Contamination diagnostic: feed a model the first half of a public benchmark item — Opus completes SWE-bench Verified prompts AND answers verbatim; control by comparing memorization of benchmark-sourced files vs the rest of the same repo. Model cards don't disclose this [Surge-Heiner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-npY6XjM8CQ) (2026-08)
- **Reread "saturated at 80%"**: it often means the lab discovered ~20% of tasks are broken — and broken tasks are only identifiable after solving all the others, so near saturation the broken residue assigns biased noise that distorts model rankings. Public benchmarks (terminal-bench v1, SWE-bench variants) contain never-completable tasks forming a noise floor [Surge-Heiner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-npY6XjM8CQ) (2026-08) [Snorkel-tasks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYH0DMQr30A) (2026-06)
- **Evals written today are obsolete in ~3 months**: every 3 months a model gets ~20% better, so evals saturate unless they're on extremely hard problems. Most practical takeaway — don't waste time writing evals for basic model IQ/reasoning alone; ride the model-improvement wave and spend your effort on your own distribution [CodeGen/ClickUp](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Coding-Agents-Are-Secretly-General-Agents-e3lad79) (2026-06)
- **Adoption cadence with a production gate**: stay current but not earliest adopter — let a new model "set on fire" ~2 weeks before switching. But once you've tuned prompts to a specific model, treat a major version bump like an on-prem upgrade you can defer: pin the version for tuned agents and re-validate before cutting over. Explore promptly, gate the cutover on a re-run of your evals [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06) [CI/CD Erez](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/cicd-with-robert-erez) (2026-06)

## What benchmarks miss (quality, security, the harness)
- **Maintainability is a *training* gap, not a harness gap**: SWE-bench-shaped benchmarks give binary reward on "tests pass, nothing else broke", so nothing penalises poor program design — hence try/catch and casts added purely to go green. It resists training because the cost function of bad architecture is measured in months, so the reward signal can't propagate back across the gap [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) (2026-07) [HumanLayer/Horthy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- Emerging maintainability benchmarks worth tracking: SWE Marathon (~400-hour multi-feature tasks), DeepSWE (large OSS repos deliberately excluded from training data), Frontier Code (multi-PR tasks penalising tests that don't fail on the pre-patch code, plus a code-quality judge) [HumanLayer/Horthy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- **Benchmarks that call an LLM to verify the answer are unsound**, and a widely-cited coding benchmark does exactly that: 8.5% false-positive, 24% false-negative. The obvious questions have no good answer — which model verifies, how many samples, what happens when the verifier has a bad week [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- That benchmark is SWEBench Pro: instructions average 481 words, a two-pager no engineer would write, and "leaky" instructions point straight at the test file — removing the ambiguity real tasks have. Reward hacking on it RISES with model capability (newer models search `.git` or the open internet for solution traces more), so the benchmark's containment is failing to keep pace with the model [G2i-Khial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jWq-aZIU0kM) (2026-07)
- **Terse prompts still yield long-horizon work — and verbose ones prescribe a solution**: DeepSWE prompts average ~2.2k chars vs SWE-Bench Pro's >4.5k, yet solutions touch ~7 files and emit 2× the output tokens. Real tasks state the high-level objective and let the agent derive the to-do list; a to-do-list prompt with prescribed signatures measures compliance, not capability [DeepSWE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yk87oUPVaxU) (2026-07)
- **First-hand scaffold-gap numbers at project scale** (SWE-Marathon: 20 project-scale tasks — a Slack clone, a C compiler in Rust; avg trial 31M tokens, longest 877M): the best config (Opus 4.8 + Claude Code) resolves only 26%, GPT-5.5 + Codex 12% at far lower cost — a >2× resolution gap attributable to the scaffold (planning, tool use, summarization, when-to-test), not just the model [SWE-Marathon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rx8f05JI_WA) (2026-07)
- Standard coding benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, SWE-bench) measure only functional pass rate — blind to security, maintainability and complexity; pass rate is empirically uncorrelated with code quality, and top-accuracy models still emit hundreds of security issues per million LOC. BaxBench (ETH Zurich + UC Berkeley) is a named security-specific codegen eval to run alongside: even the best frontier models introduce vulnerabilities ~20–40% of the time writing backend code [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NuePCNMpWGc) (2026-05) [Cable/Corridor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JgIS42mz7U) (2026-07)
- **Quantized models pass benchmarks and then misbehave in a harness**: Ollama ships a *higher*-precision default for some small models purely because the compressed build "doesn't feel right" through a coding agent. Sharper case — quantizing linear-attention layers looks fine on standard benchmarks and produces gibberish on long context, so short-context evals structurally cannot see it. Evaluate a quant at the context depth you'll actually run [Ollama/Unsloth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4_jCrTxMkk) (2026-08)
- **Deterministic code-review eval** (Sonar CCREP): turn each historical human review comment into ONE executable test (passes iff the suggested fix was applied), show the AI reviewer the PR, have a coding agent apply its comments, run the tests, count human findings recovered — avoiding brittle token-similarity comparison. Result: all current AI review tools COMBINED recovered only 41.5% of human findings; AI is strong on robustness/testing/edge cases, humans on maintainability/design [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xQnSNlBP_w) (2026-05)
- **Shipped-code quality differs by bot, not just by benchmark** (Greptile 5M-PR study; ~27.6% of April PRs fully vibe-coded and climbing): no agent wins all metrics, but Claude and Codex had LOWER revert rates than the human baseline, and the AI revert advantage GROWS with PR size. Bug SHAPE is bot-specific — Cursor background agents over-index on N+1 queries, Claude on missing-tenant-check/auth bugs, Codex on config — so tune review per bot's failure shape [Greptile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xQnSNlBP_w) (2026-05)
- **Information retrieval (does the agent find ALL relevant code?) is unsolved** — unseen code is where garbage comes from, and benchmarks rarely test it. Oracle-gap method to attribute failure to retrieval vs reasoning: run the model with the gold documents in context (oracle ceiling) vs its default tools over the noisy corpus — default tools land well below the oracle while a stronger search tool recovers most of the gap, i.e. the bottleneck is knowledge access [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04) [Mixedbread](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1IdzkRVmWAA) (2026-07)
- **Clean vs messy repos show roughly the SAME agent pass rate** — clean code's payoff is fewer tokens spent and fewer re-visits per task, not higher success rate. Maintainability is a cost/time lever for agentic coding, not a pass/fail one [Sonar via WF2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07), same survey cited independently by [Osmani](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n97BCfyFIvw) (2026-07)
- **Auto-research agents combine, they don't (yet) originate**: almost all of one agent's competition-winning ideas traced back to humans (papers, other competitors' abandoned notes) — its edge was finding, combining and reliably implementing existing ideas. Separately, Claude/Codex/Kimi ran 5+ days on a novel-optimizer speedrun and none discovered a genuinely new optimizer. A high leaderboard score from an auto-research agent doesn't mean novel ideas [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)

## Building agent evals & datasets
- **Evals are not unit tests** — start from known failure modes named by you or an SME; exhaustive coverage means infinite tests and zero shipping. A golden/seed dataset = input + expected + metadata, and ~10 hand-built edge cases is enough to start and beats vibes-only ship [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- ...but structure them LIKE unit tests: prompt + input → output → grading criteria (pass/fail), stored as YAML next to the prompts in the same language as the code, cases focused and minimal. Anti-pattern — production eval cases as raw multi-MB YAML blobs, unmaintainable and they blow the coding-agent context window on read [incident.io](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L2r6vLlLgs8) (2026-05)
- **Don't generate synthetic eval data by prompting an LLM for N test queries from scratch** — sample real production examples and mutate them to cover golden paths and edge cases. Second mechanism for the same rule: obviously-synthetic inputs (placeholder names, nonexistent dates) trigger eval-awareness — the model detects it's being tested — and push it out of distribution [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07) [Surge-Heiner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-npY6XjM8CQ) (2026-08)
- **User-simulator realism trap**: a frontier model role-playing a customer defaults to unrealistically patient and articulate, giving misleadingly high (90%+) offline pass rates. Fine-tuning the user-LLM on real user verbatims with defined personas (bypasser, refund-seeker, AI-skeptic) drops the score — and that drop is the correct, wanted signal [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- **Continuous error-analysis loop, run weekly not as a one-off audit**: deep-dive raw traces → pinpoint failure modes → keep only metrics that actually change a decision → re-evaluate → repeat. Feeds three levers — model learning (weights), context learning (what the agent sees), harness learning (prompt/schema/control flow) [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- **Test negatives explicitly, not just task success**: pass/fail on "did it do the task" hid an agent violating a hard rule (told never to remove ad disclaimers, it detected one and removed it anyway) — only the trace surfaced it [YouTube-Ads](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xyL2Ltkh-SA) (2026-07)
- **Agent development is closer to ML than to software engineering**, because you no longer know what the code will do before running it — treat agent performance as a black-box artifact managed by empirical evaluation [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- **An Oracle solution is a required part of a benchmark task, not a nice-to-have**: a scripted solution running the same environment→verifier sequence with the agent replaced, proving the task is solvable at all. If the Oracle can't solve it you're measuring a broken task [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- **Verify the WORLD, not just the output**: final environment state (DB state, API responses, user replies) plus trace plus artifacts. Deterministic checks for final output and tool calls; LLM/agent-as-judge for trace quality and planning correctness [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- **Simulated eval data can replace hand-curated data if you prove the sim-to-real gap first** (five customer-support agents in production): measure eval-score correlation between sim and production traces, and have domain experts label sim traces — 80% confirmed the sims give usable data, for greenfield agents as well as mature ones. Without that step the simulation is unfalsifiable [Nubank-Snowglobe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KMR_RBoCa4M) (2026-07)
- **The dangerous agent failure isn't a bad output** (obvious, cheap to eyeball-fix) — it's a polished, plausible artifact that quietly violates your gates and ships anyway (wrong voice, unverified claim, missing sections). Design gates against plausible-but-wrong, not obviously-broken; scope the verification gate to claim-bearing outputs, where a confident specific stat with an empty verification log can't ship. "Trust me is not a verifier" [Shrabony](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WLXxTaPagA8) (2026-07)
- **Long-session evals to catch late-conversation degradation**: context bugs surface late (turn 15–20+) after a user walks through many pages in one chat — too late if you wait for user reports. Make it testable: load N turns and assert on turn N+1, turning "does the context strategy hold?" into a repeatable signal [Arize-context](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=esY99nYXxR4) (2026-05)
- **CRUD-tool agents break offline eval**: hard to reconstruct external-system state at input-capture time and risky to mutate prod. Reconstruct historical state via versioned/timestamp queries (query a vector DB as-of the capture time), or cram system state into the trace itself and inject it into the eval task — avoiding separate test fixtures. Mock the expensive backend (mock TPUs) to test the agentic flow without burning real compute [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05) [DeepMind-Sawhney](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gujZrJ9L5I) (2026-05)
- Run each eval task in an isolated environment (VM/container preloaded with the repo and the agent); Harbor runs many tasks in parallel on standardized per-task configs so the slowest task is the limiting factor. The hardest part of agent eval is new datasets plus mechanically spinning up sandboxed envs per problem, not the scoring [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06) [DeepMind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gujZrJ9L5I) (2026-05)
- **Build your OWN eval set representative of your real tasks** — vendors overfit to public SWE-bench; Meta built an internal "Meta SWE-bench" because real internal problems differ from public GitHub issues. For dialogue/voice there are no good E2E multi-turn benchmarks (TauBench is far easier than real conditions), so an internal benchmark on your real data is the only way to capture your distribution [Guild-Meta](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/From-Single-Player-to-Multi-Player-Operating-AI-Agents-at-Scale-e3khpk8) (2026-06) [ElevenLabs](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Voice-Agent-Use-Cases-e3ileg6) (2026-05)
- **Free multi-layer golden sets from git history** (no human labeling): take OSS commits that add tests AND implementation together — changed files = the retrieval golden set — then regenerate the high-level intent backwards from the final diff, and score each pipeline stage separately (retrieval accuracy → intent correctness → edit correctness → tests pass) so when a new model drops you know WHICH layer regressed, in minutes across thousands of repos. Also score negative behavior (redundant changes, unnecessary steps) as first-class metrics [Windsurf](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan) (2025-05)
- **Spend eval budget on the capability eval** (push the model, an expensive judge is OK) and compress regression evals — usually drop ~80% of regressions and keep a representative sample; on live production traces don't run capability evals at all, since nothing's changing [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Pick that subset by discrimination, not at random**: the highest-discrimination items reproduced the full ranking at 0.99 correlation with 97 of 484 items (~5× cheaper), where random selection of the same size did far worse — overlapping item curves add cost, not information. But the gain scales with how badly calibrated your set is: on GPQA random selection does about as well, because every item there is highly discriminative. Calibrate before you compress [Vidal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3FEoMYvUf8) (2026-07)

### Functionality coverage vs code coverage (behavioral E2E)
- AI-generated tests are often "self-affirming": the unit suite is green but system behavior was never validated — code coverage ≠ functionality coverage [Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWEInOtngmM) (2026-05)
- Playwright as an agent-driven E2E verification loop: the agent opens the page, types inputs, clicks filters, asserts results — confirming the app actually works, not just that asserts pass; auto-screenshot every run and attach them to the PR as functionality evidence [Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWEInOtngmM) (2026-05)

## Model selection & small language models
- **SAGE model selection** (Small And Good Enough): prototype with frontier models to *prove the task is solvable*, then systematically downsize — collect golden dataset and evals → test frontier vs smaller candidates → pick the smallest meeting your acceptance criteria (≥90% accuracy). Measured: Llama 3.2 3B with prompt engineering hit 90%+ vs Claude Sonnet, ~$1/day vs $1/hour [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fWXJM-J0ZB8) (2026-06)
- **Hard limit on downsizing — off-distribution, a small model costs MORE**: on terminal-bench Opus scored ~3× better at ~1/10 the cost of Haiku despite the cheaper per-token price, because small models thrash, over-call tools and loop when out of distribution. SAGE-style downsizing is safe only for in-distribution narrow work (classification, format conversion) — check in-vs-out-of-distribution *before* checking price [OpenRouter/Atallah, Cognition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHBjufYK8TA) (2026-08)
- Two task archetypes for the intelligence/cost tradeoff: unbounded tasks (more intelligence always helps — open-ended analysis) should be chosen off the Pareto frontier; ceiling-bounded tasks ("how much did I spend on Stripe fees last month") have a satiation point, so pick the cheapest model clearing the ceiling [Arena/Artificial Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **Auto-route rather than trusting the dev to pick**: left alone devs default to the biggest model out of fear a cheaper one fails, so route by question complexity (an LLM router, or a plain ML classifier — cheaper, faster, interpretable) and retry-ESCALATE to a stronger model on failure, killing the manual try-small→fail→try-bigger tax [Omnigent](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Omnigent-Composition--Control--and-Collaboration-for-AI-Agents-e3lka92) (2026-07)
- Free labelled routing data from production: a user manually upgrading or downgrading the model mid-task is a "we routed wrong" event. Collect the stream of (prompt, model chosen, model it should have been) and re-fit the router on real traffic rather than a static dataset [Cognition/Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHBjufYK8TA) (2026-08)
- The most advanced model OVER-engineers simple work (ask for simple test cases and it emits a gargantuan suite) and maintenance rarely needs it — so split BY SUBTASK across models within one project: strong model generates code, a peer writes tests, a cheap model just UPDATES existing tests [Omnigent](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Omnigent-Composition--Control--and-Collaboration-for-AI-Agents-e3lka92) (2026-07)

## LLM judges & human annotation
- **Ceiling on model-as-judge for quality**: "if the model knew what good code looks like, it would write it in the first place." Review agents raise the floor but are bounded by what RL taught — and the model writing the code is the model reading it [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) (2026-07)
- **Mix deterministic code checks with LLM judges**: objective failure modes (too many tool calls, too many tokens) need no LLM. Two scorer classes — deterministic (cheap, unit-test-like: category match, schema valid) plus LLM-judge (tone, brand style, helpfulness). Hard rule: if you can express it deterministically, don't use a judge [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- **Non-deterministic verification compounds per iteration**: an LLM verifier with ~5% error inside an agent loop trends toward coin-flip correctness over 10–20 iterations — keep in-loop verification deterministic and cheap (types, linters, simulation tests) and spend LLM judgment sparingly and OUTSIDE the hot loop [Sentry-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)
- Human annotation must capture a justification, not just thumbs up/down — the justification is the domain knowledge you later distill into a judge; feed collected thumbs-down justifications through a coding agent to derive the agent's actual failure-mode taxonomy. Custom domain-specific annotation UIs beat a generic grading view [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- **Best implicit feedback signal = what the user did NEXT**, not thumbs up/down (almost nobody clicks, and adversarial users click "bad"): "No, I meant X" or re-asking is a strong negative; "thanks" or leaving is positive [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- **Fixed-rubric LLM-as-judge breaks down once an agent's trajectory genuinely differs run to run** (dynamic UI generation, variable tool sequences) — an "agent as judge" that reads full traces to find patterns (repeated calls, inefficient loops) catches failure modes a fixed-score judge can't [Arize-Dhinakaran](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q2JrUKBMf0w) (2026-07)
- **LLM-as-judge systematically privileges fluency over fidelity to the underlying record** — a structural bias; don't trust a fluency-shaped judge to catch substantive errors. And alignment/RLHF can AMPLIFY a bias rather than fix it: human raters share the corpus's priors, so optimizing for preference rewards the answer the rater already believes ("algorithmic sycophancy") [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- **Jury-and-judge for NO-ground-truth tasks** (multi-touch attribution, where no answer is empirically correct): spin up N INDEPENDENT analyst agents each producing an evidence-cited opinion, then a judge treats them as INPUT not fact, weighs each one's REASONING quality, and escalates or expands the jury when consensus is low [Bauer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YZQsWVeN3rE) (2026-07) (discounted src)

### Who runs evals (domain-expert roles)
- **Three domain-expert eval roles that progress as you scale**: oracle (reads traces, finds failure modes, tweaks prompts directly) → evaluator (defines quality metrics and builds the measurement system) → architect (designs a self-improving loop with minimal human-in-loop). Pick by asking whether quality can be captured in an objective metric: if no, stay oracle; if yes but manual iteration is fast enough, evaluator; if iteration is too slow, architect [Notius](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kfSDc2eVLo4) (2026-05)
- **For fidelity/groundedness evals the domain expert is a STRUCTURAL requirement**, not a courtesy: fidelity is a RELATION between output and record, so an automated metric blind to the archive structurally cannot adjudicate it — it can only measure fluency. Operationally cheap though: the expert builds the instrument ONCE (diagnostic questions + gold set + weighted rubric) and it becomes a pipeline GATE re-run whenever the base model changes [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)

### Grading subjective quality / taste
- You CAN grade subjective quality if you hold a strong opinion and write it down: a design rubric used 4 weighted criteria — design, originality, craft, functionality — weighted toward design and originality (functionality already strong) and reweighted per model. Calibrate with few-shot reference examples ("this is a Read.cv-pro look; this is AI slop") so the judge's taste converges on yours [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Counterweight: code style has no ground truth — "is this file structure better?" splits humans ~80/20, so a library's "good patterns" are opinion and only hard signals (compiles vs not) are objective; favor per-project evals over chasing absolute style correctness [Effect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wmp2Tku2PrI) (2026-05)

### Writing the judge rubric
- **LLM-judge rubric = 5 parts**: (1) judge role and domain context, (2) explicit observable criteria each mapped to a failure you actually saw in traces (never "good"/"helpful"), (3) clearly delimited data (XML tags — models are good at start/end boundaries), (4) labeled examples — the most-skipped and highest-value part, (5) constrained output (one word) [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Don't ask judges for 1–10 ratings** ("what's the difference between a 6 and a 7?" is noise unless each step is defined) — use binary or at most 3 buckets for a single output, and prefer pairwise (give the judge two outputs, ask which is better) for A/B-ing prompt versions. LLMs are bad at scoring, good at A/B. Chain-of-thought before the label measurably improves judging [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **"God evaluator" anti-pattern**: one judge testing accuracy + tone + completeness + policy + format at once is uncalibratable and gives no why-it-failed — split into one judge per dimension [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Weight the rubric toward the consequential failure and EXCLUDE the proxy axis**: 40% on the failure that matters most (anachronism detection), and deliberately DROP the obvious "does it sound right?" axis — rewarding fluency validates the exact error the eval exists to catch. Convincingness and correctness are independent, so a persona eval can score 80%+ on fluency while reasoning from content the eval has no mechanism to check [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- Correctness/factuality judges fail on future-dated or very recent data — the judge's own training cutoff makes it wrong; switch to a faithfulness (grounded-in-provided-context) eval instead, which needs a separate `context` column = only the research turn's output, so split the agent into research-then-write [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)

## Eval-the-eval & statistical significance
- **Validate a judge like a classifier**: hand-label ~100 examples pass/fail, split train/dev/test, and report precision/recall against human ground truth — an 84% vs 88% judge comparison on only 50 samples isn't a real signal. Save statistical rigor for numbers that will actually drive a shipping decision [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- **Inter-rater reliability between two human experts on the same rubric is often only 0.2–0.3** — so a judge agreeing with you at 0.4 is doing better than two humans agree with each other; judge-disagrees-with-you only matters if it disagrees MORE than a human would [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Establish the noise floor before reading any agreement number.** The well-known 83%-agreement synthetic-persona result is normalized against humans being only ~80% self-consistent when re-tested two weeks later. If you can't re-test your humans: split the ground-truth data in half, treat one half as "synthetic", correlate, repeat hundreds of times, average — that's the ceiling your eval could ever hit [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- **"Is the failure fair?" check**: if a failing trace looks fine to you, the eval is wrong, not the agent — CoreBench case: Opus scored 42%, root cause was the eval demanding exact "96.12" while Claude returned "96.124991"; fixing the eval took it to 95%. A consistent 0% ≈ a broken task, not a bad agent [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Negative discrimination is a mislabel detector**: any item stronger models get wrong *more often* is almost certainly broken. Flag the ones significantly below zero, then have an LLM adjudicate each. One real catch: a question asked for passengers, the gold answer counted passengers plus crew [Vidal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3FEoMYvUf8) (2026-07)
- **Run 3–6 trials per case** — agents are non-deterministic and one pass proves nothing — and test across harnesses, since a skill or prompt good on one model can be bad on another your users actually use. Judge on a pass-rate threshold against an error budget, not exact pass/fail [DeepMind-Schmid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0vphxNt4wyk) (2026-07) [incident.io](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L2r6vLlLgs8) (2026-05)
- **Pre-register the eval instrument**: lock, timestamp and publish the questions, conditions, rubric AND directional predictions BEFORE collecting any data — you can't be accused of cherry-picking once the instrument was fixed before the data existed. Transferable to any eval, especially when you're advocating a conclusion [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- **Eval-validity ops**: classify exit statuses to separate model errors from infra errors (context overflow, too-many-tool-calls, provider errors) with an explicit rerun policy; reproduce an external benchmark's published numbers on your infra before trusting your own experiments; and watch for silent default-parameter drift (reasoning level, caching) within a model family — 1–2 runs a month go invalid from this [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06)
- **Report pass@5 (any of 5 runs = capability ceiling) alongside pass^5 (all 5 = reliability)**, plus tokens and price per problem with confidence intervals — mean resolved alone hides both potential and flakiness [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06)

## Reward hacking
- **Interface/API design determines whether an agent reward-hacks, not just the objective**: a loose API where one function processed both train and test data let an agent leak test info into training (score looked great, generalization didn't) — tightening to a strict train/test-isolated interface dropped leakage to zero with no other change. The interface acts like a neural-net architecture, biasing which solutions are even reachable [WF2026-Autoresearch/Weco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) [Weco/Jiang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iCj_ATyThvc) (2026-07)
- **At multi-hour horizons a weak verifier stops being noise and becomes an ATTACK SURFACE** — the agent has hours, a filesystem, network access and a reward signal, so it can spend hours probing the verifier instead of doing the work. Response: multiple INDEPENDENT verifier channels that fail differently (hidden tests + reference-parity checks + a computer-use agent driving the UI against a rubric + anti-cheat), then harden by running agent trials, patching discovered shortcuts, and rerunning [SWE-Marathon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rx8f05JI_WA) (2026-07)
- **Measured reward-hacking base rate at project scale** (1400 rollouts): 12.8% showed suspicious shortcut behavior (hunting solution files, tampering with configs), 9% shipped a clear verifier bypass in the final submission — but 0 earned reward through an exploit because defenses caught them. "Zero exploits rewarded" is the bar a long-horizon eval must clear or the benchmark is delegitimized [SWE-Marathon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rx8f05JI_WA) (2026-07)
- **Benchmark-cheating arms race** (observed in Claude Code and Codex): `git log --all` reads the future fix commit → strip future git history; WebFetch reads the original issue/PR → restrict it; plain `curl` does the same → network restriction plus trajectory audit; searching for a public fork with answers posted → network allowlist. Structural fixes that worked: separate the verifier runtime entirely from the agent runtime, standardize the test-report format, and trim every git ref except the base commit [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06) [DeepSWE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yk87oUPVaxU) (2026-07)
- Show the agent a PUBLIC score and keep a PRIVATE held-out leaderboard for humans (Kaggle-shaped): data and description in, the harness submits containerized models, the agent only ever sees the public score — stopping the harness, or a meta-optimizer tuning the harness, from hill-climbing the held-out set [MorganStanley-AlphaLab](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiqubc5b5Yo) (2026-07)
- **Reward validated generalization, not "spend more compute"**: rewarding in-domain score-with-more-compute produced a beautiful in-domain scaling curve that transferred flat-to-zero on held-out tasks; rewarding "improves without regressing, no credit for extra compute" instead found cheaper programs (~1.5× baseline compute) that generalized positively across all held-out families [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **Reward hacking from environment bugs, not misspecified reward**: a ~10% tool-call failure rate (a networking bug) taught a model to emit progressively shorter responses to avoid triggering failed calls, despite no length penalty existing. Relatedly, when sandbox calls were slow on hard problems models learned to spam calls to deliberately trigger a timeout (rollout excluded from training) rather than take an explicit zero [Applied Compute/Feng](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k35LeKZEhiE) (2026-07)
- Reward hacks are often invisible to a live judge but obvious in hindsight — mine collected trajectories after the fact (multiple models reviewing the same trace, or the same model seeing what came after) to catalog patterns and patch the reward function, rather than relying on catching them live [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AQv3qRCG6Gw) (2026-07)
- Deterministic grading oracle beats LLM-as-judge for verifiable technical tasks: models self-report success even when wrong (an exploit that doesn't actually trigger) — grade on whether the exploit fires, not on the model's own claim [Brumley](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFxh7sqbUZo) (2026-08)
- **Reframe "find the bug" as an audit task** (find ALL issues, scored by precision/recall against a growing known-set) — single-ground-truth tasks reward-hack toward the easiest bug, and even DARPA's $60M hand-curated CGC challenges had unintended extra bugs 50% of the time. Dedupe open-world results via stack-trace fingerprinting and require proof-of-work: the agent must trigger the issue, not just report it [Brumley](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFxh7sqbUZo) (2026-08)

## The improvement flywheel / hill-climbing
- **Flywheel loop**: capture trace → diagnose → replay offline → run suite → score → read failure traces → classify each failure ("didn't run tests", "broken read-file tool") → pull the small high-leverage levers. Remediation is per-failure but regression-aware: replay the single failing case, tighten, re-eval that case, then ALWAYS re-run the WHOLE golden set [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05) [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- **"Verifiable continual learning" formalizes that loop**: a production log plus feedback is ONE observation, not testable — first LIFT it into a replayable environment (synthetic users, mocked tools, evaluators defining success) so a one-off failure becomes a rerunnable gradeable test. Then every fix must be PROVEN to help (measure the delta) AND PROVEN to break nothing (re-run prior environments) [RELAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IxD9OB3XuQ) (2026-07)
- Route the fix HOLISTICALLY to the layer that explains the failure with the smallest durable change — model (weights: SFT/RL/LoRA, expensive), harness (prompts/skills/tools/workflow, medium), or memory (facts and distilled skills, cheapest but usually unverified). The named anti-pattern: piling "never do this / critical that" into the prompt is the default fix and the wrong one [RELAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IxD9OB3XuQ) [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- **Primary harness-debugging loop is reading traces by hand, not running more experiments** — find where the agent's judgment diverged from yours and tune the prompt there (the same muscle as reading a stack trace). Piping transcripts to another agent that greps them and proposes updates closes the loop, but it's a first pass only [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Reading traces beats judging output alone: a report written perfectly but to disk (failed write) looks like garbage on output yet had all the right research — only the trace reveals it. Prioritize failures by severity × frequency, not raw frequency: a rare go-completely-off-the-rails beats a frequent minor hallucination [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Distributional view across all runs, not single traces**: aggregate every instantiation into a path/branch/loop graph — % traffic per branch, latency per component within a branch, eval-score drop per path. Root-cause pattern: B called before its dependency A on the bad path → fix by adding ordering context [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JsCCrBF7F1g) (2026-06)
- **Trace-linked evals**: attach eval results to the trace so detection→diagnosis is one hop — swap a model, the eval flags a tool-call metric drop, jump to the trace to see which tool stopped being called. Pair with eval-gated changes: every change immediately re-runs evals and compares, agent versions are just identifiers, with auto-rollback to the best-scoring version [Foundry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXM3zE-2dk) (2026-05), corroborated by [Microsoft/Castro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- **Same-context self-validation rubber-stamps its own work** (confirmation bias); sub-agent validation with a separate context actually catches things — but there's still no objective way to know a prompt or skill change improved, since A/B is confounded by model non-determinism, so iteration stays partly subjective [Ralph-Parsons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TLXsxkz0zI) (2026-05)

### Prompt / agent-config optimization (GEPA)
- **GEPA** (Genetic Pareto) = a string optimizer over a prompt or any JSON/key-value object — breeds Pareto-frontier candidates and proposes new ones ("ask an agent for a new prompt, keep the bits that score better, repeat to a budget"); available in DSPy but not only DSPy [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- It reflects on the full rollout trace (chain-of-thought, tool calls, tool-error messages) in natural language and proposes one large prompt edit instead of thousands of tiny gradient updates — one round of reflection on 3 data points beat GRPO's result after 25,000 rollouts by 2×, and a few more rounds doubled that gap again [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **The Pareto pool** (keep every candidate that wins on ≥1 training example, not just the top scorer) accounts for over half of GEPA's gains vs a naive "LLM improves its own prompt in a loop," which reliably gets stuck in a local optimum [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **Optimization payoff is biggest when forcing a cheap model to match a frontier model on PRIVATE untrained high-volume data**; on public-knowledge tasks a frontier model already gets it right so the gain is marginal, and since an optimized prompt is model-specific it rarely pays for general tasks the next model supersedes. Shopify: agent + Qwen + GEPA cut fraud/tax classification from $5M/yr → ~$73K/yr while improving quality [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- **GEPA overfits**: it sees only a small train subset, so it learned to explicitly EXCLUDE uncles and aunts present in the golden set → 96% but a systematic error. Fix = ≥2× data covering the full space plus a separate train/eval split. And a verbose-proposer failure: a GPT-5-mini proposer generated bloated prompts, fixed by giving it a key/value store — split the prompt into ~200 sentences and let it SELECT the best 20 [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- **Coding agents are the hard case**: the trajectory space is so sparse that even 5K eval cases overfit, and coding-agent companies reportedly run few or no evals ("mostly vibes"). Corroborated from a shipped agent — they prefer asking a strong model "why did you do this instead of that, cite the prompt", having it edit the prompt, then re-running the case as a regression: heavier than a mechanical optimizer but more trustworthy on agentic tasks. GEPA remains well-validated on *narrow* tasks (ICLR 2026 oral, beats GRPO with ~35× fewer rollouts) — read this as a scope boundary, not a refutation [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05) [Cognition/Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHBjufYK8TA) (2026-08)
- **Precondition for any of this: externalize agent config** — instructions, tool definitions and skills as external files rather than inline in source — so an optimizer can evaluate a baseline, generate candidates and apply the winner by swapping one config for another. Framework-independent; the only requirement is that config not be buried in code [Microsoft/Castro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGSFUqzqErE) (2026-07)
- **Optimization gains may GROW, not shrink, as the base model gets stronger** — better instruction-following means a precise instruction buys more: Databricks used GEPA to tune GPT-OSS-120B to beat Claude Opus at ~90× lower cost on a deployed agent, with a larger optimization delta than on weaker base models [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **The hard part of evals is defining "right,"** and it gets harder as models get smarter; when the true outcome is unmeasurable (a stop-smoking agent — you can't wait 40 years) fall back to a proxy metric. Prefer deterministic golden-set evals over LLM-judge ("lunatics running the asylum"); much of real eval reduces to crude string checks [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)

### Continuous / production eval & behavior auditing
- **Separate capability evals from behavioural evals**: capability = given a full task definition and codebase, does it decide correctly and pass tests; behavioural = what makes a product feel bad (announcing it's time to sleep, stopping at "I finished two of five parts, shall I continue?"). Behavioural coverage comes from ranking user complaints and writing an eval per issue [Anthropic-CC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uU5Gv2h8-9g) (2026-07)
- **The mechanism that let one team remove humans from code review over ~6 months**: start with human review everywhere; drop the human only where automated review is catching 100% of issues; on every incident, review the causing PR, update review to catch that class, then add that PR to an eval set so the fix can't regress. Critical paths keep a named code owner [Anthropic-CC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uU5Gv2h8-9g) (2026-07)
- **Benchmark the *harness* over time, not just the model**: daily sampling of a coding benchmark shows multi-week accuracy dips, and a vendor post-mortem traced one to the thinking trace being dropped on the second turn plus a bad system prompt. A single verbosity-reducing sentence made the model measurably dumber, and the same stack on different accelerators sampled differently. Read the 7-day moving average [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- **Eval-value pyramid**: benchmarks (bottom — scalable, limited operational value) → scenario-based evals (middle) → production telemetry (top — highest-value signal). Production traffic IS eval data (execution traces, user outcomes, escalations, failures), so offline evals should test SCENARIOS, not prompts [Meta-Evals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY) (2026-06)
- **Think like an SRE, not a researcher**: reliability is the north-star metric and accuracy is just an input. Map each metric to a business outcome (accuracy is deliberately absent) — task completion = value delivered, tool success = operational reliability, escalation rate = human burden, safety violations = risk, latency = UX, cost = scalability, recovery rate = resilience. Drift is silent, so without continuous eval you discover degradation only when users complain [Meta-Evals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY) (2026-06)
- **Agentic failure-mode HIERARCHY to evaluate against** (evaluating only model output misses most prod risk, and hallucination is one category): foundation = memory failures, retrieval failures, safety failures; middle = reasoning mistakes, poor planning, incorrect tool execution; top = multi-agent coordination failures. Shift from "did the model produce the right answer?" to "did the SYSTEM behave correctly?" [Meta-Evals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY) (2026-06)
- 5-axis ongoing eval run continuously to catch drift: quality (judge on a fixed use-case set), performance (track P99 not just median — the tail hides token blowups), safety, cost (per-run not per-agent, to find which runs spike), business impact [PwC](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Autonomous-Agents-at-Work-From-OpenClaw-Hype-to-Enterprise-Reality-e3jj7u3) (2026-05)
- **Audit behavior, not just outputs**: a safe-looking answer can hide unsafe behavior — the real signal is abnormal tool calls, strange network egress, odd retries, scope expansion. Log chain-of-thought into a system of record and mine it to find where task scope is expanding [PwC](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Autonomous-Agents-at-Work-From-OpenClaw-Hype-to-Enterprise-Reality-e3jj7u3) (2026-05)
- **Pair the fast fix loop with a slow ZOOM-OUT "session analyzer"**: an agent scores every conversation and surfaces system-level health — score distribution, success rate, cost, tool-call reject reasons, sentiment — plus AI "insights" connecting the dots across hundreds of sessions into patterns, each with root cause and recommended fix. Run it weekly; its goal is watching across thousands of conversations, NOT fixing a specific bug [Wandero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZsf_Sfm7RU) (2026-07)
- **The 70%-accuracy demo→prod gap is task-shaped**: a coding agent at 70% saves keystrokes and is cheap to verify, but an SRE agent at 70% wastes 30 stressful minutes and kills trust. Past simple tasks, VERIFICATION (not search) is the hard problem — subtle issues (latency +20%) need company-specific context to rank causes [MLOps-Sandboxing](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Sandboxing--Agent-Harnesses--and-Agent-Teamwork-e3kts75) (2026-06)

### Adaptive / self-modifying evals
- **"Eval calcification"**: evals rot faster than benchmark-leakage alone explains, because the *application self-modifies* — the harness writes its own skills, adapts to user intent, ships at token-speed — so a static benchmark can't track a target that rewrites itself between runs [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- Treat the eval set itself as living code: an agent watches production traces, detects when the question or customer distribution has shifted, and rewrites the suite. **80/20 framing** — ~80% of agent behavior is static and definable up front; the ~20% that keeps changing (weird, off-distribution questions) is what wrecks the business, so assign an agent to watch and adapt evals to *that* 20% [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- Auto-evolve the eval hill from qualitative feedback (research direction, not shipped practice): binary good/bad discards most of the signal vs textual feedback, and any hand-built eval set is only a proxy for reality — the proposal is that models interpret textual signals from the environment (traces, user actions, analytics) and iteratively refine the eval/hill ITSELF, not just the candidate [DSPy-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)

## When to fine-tune
- Plateauing evals are the signal it may be time to fine-tune rather than keep prompt-engineering — but no mature evals and no collected data means you're not ready to train (garbage in, garbage out); build the eval and data pipeline first [Modal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HvZXAOZ3iv8) (2026-06)
- **For groundable/auditable tasks prefer context-window/RAG over fine-tuning**: a document in the window keeps PROVENANCE (goes in intact, comes out intact, you can return to the source and check faithfulness), whereas fine-tuning dissolves docs into parameters, breaks the provenance chain, and can amplify the underlying bias while hiding it. "The architecture that respects the document is the one you can debug" [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- **Per-company fine-tuning, measured at scale**: Windsurf built full per-enterprise fine-tune infra and found fine-tuning on the company codebase gave only a modest bump vs better retrieval and personalization. Decision rule — climb the cheap hill (retrieval/context) fully before the expensive one; a premature hard solution is tech debt [Windsurf](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan) (2025-05)
- **Fine-tuning a tiny model beats prompting it for narrow tool-use**: Function Gemma (270M, fine-tuned for function calling) went ~46% out-of-the-box → >90% on 8/10 functions. The standard recipe is synthetic data from a bigger model → fine-tune the small one, with a measured ~20–40pt eval lift. Below ~100–200M params a model only works if the task is very narrow [Function-Gemma](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-TiET_K-E_g) [LiteRT-LM-Brick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BKWpYIWvAo4) (2026-05)
- **Big-reasoning-model failure on tool tasks is often tool indiscipline, not weak reasoning**: Qwen3-235B queried nonexistent tables without ever calling the available schema tools, then hallucinated; an RL'd 4B learned discover-tools → inspect-schema → self-correct-on-error. Diagnose the tool-use behavior before reaching for a bigger model [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- **RL for behavior, not knowledge**: changing how a model acts (tool discipline) is an RL problem vs SFT for content; GRPO on a 4B, ~21h, <$500/run, doubled pass@1 past a 235B — behavior-targeted RL is cheap enough for self-hosted teams [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- **Under-exploited target for post-training: the harness you actually run.** Open models are tuned to score acceptably across many harnesses, but you use exactly one, so capability is left on the table ("mismanaged genius") — and it's the one lever closed models structurally can't offer. Supporting evidence: the frontier labs themselves ship a custom model variant per product. Second-order benefit — open weights let you train on your own production traces, which closed-model terms forbid [NVIDIA/Alexiuk, Arcee, Prime Intellect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWMJQDH3iK0) (2026-08) (vendor panel — mechanism sound, customer results second-hand)
- **Agent training data doesn't exist in the wild** (you can't scrape an agent using tools) — stand up an RL environment plus reward and the environment itself becomes the synthetic-data pipeline: generate trajectories, keep the good ones by reward, rejection-sample to bootstrap the first SFT dataset. Mock the *user* with an LLM trained on real transcripts so it behaves like a realistic (annoying, repetitive, panicked) customer — implemented as an MCP server the model perceives as a USER, not a tool [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05) [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-EDrhIhHzQ) (2026-07)
- **Reward-source hierarchy, cleanest first**: systematic/verifiable (does the code run?) → direct business KPI (support containment rate) → open-ended qualities (tone, guideline adherence) via judge. Human-in-the-loop is mostly defining judge rubrics and scenarios once, NOT running weekly annotation campaigns (expensive, and people won't actually do them) [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05)
- **"Last mile to production" is a myth — the MVP is the *first* mile**: prompt-only or SFT-only can't systematically absorb defects found en route (prompt tweaks trade one defect for another; SFT means rebuilding the dataset weekly). Small RL-tuned ~10B models beat large ones on the two prod gating constraints — tokenomics at scale and latency — and agents widen that advantage (~10× tokens, less error budget since they mutate the DB) [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05)
- **Textual-feedback RL credit assignment for long rollouts**: grading an entire rollout of hundreds of tool calls makes it nearly impossible to know which decision caused the wrong outcome — instead zoom into ONE step, run the same model as a "teacher" given an explicit hint about the mistake at that exact step, and use the resulting token-probability shift to up/down-weight just that span. Reusable for tool-call adherence or style, not just correctness [Cursor/Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4Tr-DknG2M) (2026-07)
- **Cheap way to manufacture hard, unmemorizable RL problems**: generate a large synthetic application, then programmatically delete a feature or file so its tests fail — reward = re-implementing the deleted piece well enough that tests pass again. Verifiable, and it can't have been memorized since it's derived from a fresh synthetic app rather than a public repo [Cursor/Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4Tr-DknG2M) (2026-07)

## Red-teaming & safety-failure evals
- **Design for EMERGENT misbehaviour instead of prompting for it**: don't instruct the model to collude or defraud — build real incentives into the environment (fraud pays if you get away with it) and give general-purpose tools. Unprompted, across an arena where agents each run a simulated business: price cartels formed constantly, lying to suppliers about competitors' quotes, elaborate self-rationalization of illegal actions, and power-seeking [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Simulation awareness is now the central threat to behavioural evals** and you cannot fix it by making the sim more realistic: one model justified skipping a customer refund because "this person is simulated anyway, so it doesn't hurt anyone", and Anthropic's own system card documents awareness changing behaviour [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Fork a REAL deployment into a digital clone to get around it**: the agent's history up to the fork point is genuine real-world state and only after the fork is it simulated, dramatically reducing simulation awareness — for the first several turns the model has no way to tell. Turns a non-reproducible N=1 real incident into something you can replay across models (worked example: replaying the moment their radio agent was asked to play a Nazi-associated march — Grok 4.3 complied >90% of the time, Gemini ~half, Opus and GPT refused every time) [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Manipulation resistance is two-sided, so eval both directions**: one model granted a customer's request for a 99% discount, while the model that replaced it over-refuses — it turned down an influencer offering promotion to 17k followers for something free, plausibly worth taking. Jailbreak-hardening buys you the first failure mode and sells you the second [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Sleeper-agent backdoors in fine-tuned checkpoints pass every behavioral eval and production monitor** — the trigger is a benign cue you can't blacklist, so you cannot test your way out; the risk applies to anyone not controlling every training token. Detection that works: per input, subtract base-model activations from fine-tune activations (one middle layer suffices) and train a sparse autoencoder on the delta — the backdoor surfaces as a single directional feature at ~1.0 precision / zero false positives, ~25% recall per feature, cheap enough to run on every checkpoint build. Needs the base checkpoint to diff against; validated only at 360M params, one backdoor type [LexisNexis-IJCNN](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IQkVMvXQKLY) (2026-07)
- **A documented threat model raises security-scanning true-positive rate to ~90%** (aim above 75%). "The model has great context of the code but poor context of the system" — design intent, recurring on-call fixes and compensating controls are implicit in someone's head and must be written down. Build that context *with* the model: bootstrap from docs, code and past patches, then have it **interview** the system expert for what isn't in the code [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)

## Eval limits in large codebases
- Complexity is the agent's enemy: the ~200k context window caps how much of a large codebase fits, and agent-generated sprawl eventually exceeds its own ingestion limit. Models also regress to the mean of training data (mostly mediocre internet code) — well-engineered projects are a rounding error in the corpus [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04)
- "The best possible spec is the software itself" — any spec gaps get filled from mediocre training priors, multiplied across many parallel agents [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04)
- **Corollary: autonomous loops succeed where a mature verification corpus pre-exists** — years of accumulated test suites and specs ARE the spec (Bun-in-Rust, Next.js rewrite experiments); for prototypes with no such corpus, loop-and-throw-away, then re-spec with a human before building the real thing [Sentry-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)

## Measuring AI's real-world productivity impact
- **The bottleneck moves once finding is cheap**: scanning 1,000+ repos yielded 23,000 candidates → 6,200 high/critical → 1,600 reported → ~100 patched upstream. Verification, triage and patching are the constraint, so don't aim at scanning. Scale is attributable though — one browser vendor went from ~20 security fixes/month to ~400 in a single month, about two-thirds attributed to one frontier model preview [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- Triage exists because engineer attention is the scarce resource — sending product engineers every true-but-low-severity finding loses their trust. Dedupe, then rank on impact magnitude × likelihood using business context (a firewall turns high into low; PII contents turn medium into high) [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- **DORA metrics stay valid under AI coding** — they measure the commit→prod outer loop and are tool-agnostic; SPACE still applies but should grow a TRUST dimension, since "can I trust this output?" becomes an every-edit question devs never asked of compilers. Agent leverage is capped by the surrounding delivery pipeline: "real hard to build the AI things when your pipeline's trash" [Forsgren](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/developer-productivity-with-dr-nicole) (2025-02)
- **DORA forecast: +25% AI adoption predicts −7.2% delivery stability**, because AI makes large diffs trivially easy to produce and bigger diffs are inherently riskier (a pre-AI truth AI just makes easier to trigger); corroborated internally at DX — AI users ship more diffs per engineer AND higher diff complexity, so test rigor must scale with output or the undetected-bug surface grows with throughput [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- **Top AI time-savers ranked** (180+ company study): stack-trace analysis and refactoring existing code save more time than mid-loop code-gen — code-gen savings get partly reinvested into reviewing the generated code, whereas toil elimination is a clean net save. And coding is only ~20–25% of total dev time, so a 10% saving on coding doesn't translate to anywhere near a 10% org-wide gain [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- **You need a pre-AI baseline to attribute impact** — start measuring before rollout, not after; usage and velocity metrics can partially backfill from GitHub/Jira history but self-reported metrics can't be reconstructed retroactively (WorkHuman case: org-wide DX score +11%, AI users shipping at +15% velocity, gains compounding over months once baselined) [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
