# Evals

## Reading benchmark numbers
- **The harness decides the score**: the same benchmark measured ~40% via one CLI agent vs ~50% via a controlled harness, and ~20% vs ~40% for another. A single number under a single scaffold is **one sample from a distribution nobody measured**; cross-harness differencing is the primary cause of benchmark divergence [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) [State of Data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyIoTOAbRfs) (2026-07). See harnesses.md § What the harness is
- Inference providers are "throughput maxing, accuracy minimizing": across providers serving the *same* open-weight model a ~10-point spread was measured (76.4% vs 62.4%). Check your provider's accuracy before adopting — open weights get blamed for what the provider did [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- **Benchmark bugs are mundane and huge**: a maths benchmark correction moved a frontier model from ~50% to ~80%, caused by answer-extraction errors (dropped minus signs, off-by-ones). Fixing extraction alone raised one open model 10%→25%. Low open-weight scores are often formatting, not capability [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- **"Don't let the test writer grade the task."** The dominant contrived-data recipe: hire domain experts, chat-generate plausible tasks, cherry-pick where the model diverged, package as a hard north-star benchmark — then sell the training data that hill-climbs it. Stop outsourcing your definition of realism to the vendors you buy evals from [State of Data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyIoTOAbRfs) (2026-07)
- **Counting right answers assumes every question is equally important.** Item response theory fits per-item difficulty and discrimination and estimates ability on a shared scale. On real epoch.ai data Opus 4.1 scored 245/337 and Gemini 3 Pro 247/337 — a tie by raw count, ~1 SD apart in IRT ability [Vidal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3FEoMYvUf8) (2026-07), corroborated by [arXiv 2505.15055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15055)
- **Read METR-style time-horizon curves at the 80–90%+ success line, not 50%** — the exponential trend survives, but you stop claiming models complete 18-hour tasks. Decision rule: a 1-hour agent run with a 50% completion chance most likely costs you the hour AND the attention [Wisedocs-Linkov](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vn4WpqNpck) (2026-08)
- **Time-splits are the only real decontamination**: released questions and solutions leak into next-gen pretraining, so collect only fresh tasks from the previous month and re-evaluate monthly. Diagnostic — feed a model the first half of a public item; Opus completes SWE-bench Verified prompts AND answers verbatim [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06) [Surge-Heiner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-npY6XjM8CQ) (2026-08)
- **The replay-agent exploit — a static benchmark is gameable by construction**: record one successful trajectory per task, compile them into a <1MB script that blindly replays the action sequence, and on OSWorld / MobileWorld it MATCHES OR BEATS the model it came from. If a blind script ties your frontier model, the benchmark measures determinism [Programma-D'Oro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08261) (2026-08)
- ...and the corollary kills a common metric: **on a deterministic environment pass@k is formally equivalent to scoring that replay agent** (proved), so it rewards search budget over capability. Read pass@k as a capability ceiling only where the ENVIRONMENT varies run to run [Programma-D'Oro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08261) (2026-08)
- **Reread "saturated at 80%"**: it often means ~20% of tasks are broken — and broken tasks are identifiable only after solving the others, so near saturation the broken residue assigns biased noise that distorts rankings. terminal-bench v1 and SWE-bench variants contain never-completable tasks [Surge-Heiner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-npY6XjM8CQ) (2026-08) [Snorkel-tasks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYH0DMQr30A) (2026-06)
- **The specific thing that rots is TRAJECTORY assertions**: "must call tool X for this question" dies the moment you change harness (~80% of one suite broke moving to Claude Code CLI) or model. Assert on outcomes — and don't delay a model upgrade two weeks to rewrite an eval set [Raindrop-Hylak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jHMiYtjoJfA) (2026-08)
- **Adoption cadence with a production gate**: stay current but not earliest adopter — let a new model "set on fire" ~2 weeks. But once prompts are tuned to a model, treat a major version bump like an on-prem upgrade you can defer: pin the version and re-validate before cutting over [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) [CI/CD Erez](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/cicd-with-robert-erez) (2026-06)

## What benchmarks miss (quality, security, the harness)
- **Maintainability is a *training* gap, not a harness gap**: SWE-bench-shaped benchmarks give binary reward on "tests pass, nothing else broke", so nothing penalises poor design — hence try/catch and casts added purely to go green. It resists training because bad architecture's cost is measured in months, so the reward signal can't propagate back [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) [HumanLayer/Horthy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- **Benchmarks that call an LLM to verify the answer are unsound**, and SWEBench Pro does exactly that: 8.5% false-positive, 24% false-negative. Its instructions average 481 words and point straight at the test file, and reward hacking on it RISES with model capability (newer models search `.git` or the internet for solution traces) [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) [G2i-Khial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jWq-aZIU0kM) (2026-07)
- **Terse prompts still yield long-horizon work — verbose ones prescribe a solution**: DeepSWE prompts average ~2.2k chars vs SWE-Bench Pro's >4.5k, yet solutions touch ~7 files and emit 2× the output tokens. A to-do-list prompt with prescribed signatures measures compliance, not capability [DeepSWE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yk87oUPVaxU) (2026-07)
- **First-hand scaffold-gap numbers at project scale** (SWE-Marathon: 20 project-scale tasks, avg trial 31M tokens, longest 877M): the best config (Opus 4.8 + Claude Code) resolves only 26%, GPT-5.5 + Codex 12% at far lower cost — a >2× gap attributable to the scaffold (planning, tool use, summarization, when-to-test) [SWE-Marathon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rx8f05JI_WA) (2026-07)
- Pass rate is empirically uncorrelated with code quality, so run a security-specific codegen eval (BaxBench) alongside the standard ones: even the best frontier models introduce vulnerabilities ~20–40% of the time writing backend code [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NuePCNMpWGc) (2026-05) [Cable/Corridor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JgIS42mz7U) (2026-07)
- **Quantized models pass benchmarks and then misbehave in a harness**: Ollama ships a *higher*-precision default for some small models purely because the compressed build "doesn't feel right" through a coding agent. Quantizing linear-attention layers looks fine on standard benchmarks and produces gibberish on long context — evaluate a quant at the depth you'll run [Ollama/Unsloth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4_jCrTxMkk) (2026-08)
- **Deterministic code-review eval** (Sonar CCREP): turn each historical human review comment into ONE executable test (passes iff the fix was applied), show the AI reviewer the PR, have a coding agent apply its comments, run the tests, count human findings recovered. All current AI review tools COMBINED recovered only 41.5%; AI is strong on robustness/edge cases, humans on maintainability/design [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xQnSNlBP_w) (2026-05)
- **Shipped-code quality differs by bot, not just by benchmark** (Greptile 5M-PR study; ~27.6% of April PRs fully vibe-coded): Claude and Codex had LOWER revert rates than the human baseline, and the AI advantage GROWS with PR size. Bug SHAPE is bot-specific — Cursor background agents over-index on N+1 queries, Claude on missing-tenant-check bugs, Codex on config [Greptile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xQnSNlBP_w) (2026-05)
- **Information retrieval (does the agent find ALL relevant code?) is unsolved** and benchmarks rarely test it. Oracle-gap method to attribute failure to retrieval vs reasoning: run with the gold documents in context (oracle ceiling) vs default tools over the noisy corpus — default tools land well below the oracle, i.e. the bottleneck is knowledge access [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04) [Mixedbread](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1IdzkRVmWAA) (2026-07)
- **Clean vs messy repos show roughly the SAME agent pass rate** — clean code's payoff is fewer tokens spent and fewer re-visits per task, not higher success. Maintainability is a cost/time lever for agentic coding, not a pass/fail one [Sonar via WF2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07), cited independently by [Osmani](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n97BCfyFIvw) (2026-07)
- **Auto-research agents combine, they don't (yet) originate**: almost all of one agent's competition-winning ideas traced back to humans (papers, other competitors' abandoned notes) — its edge was finding, combining and reliably implementing. Claude/Codex/Kimi ran 5+ days on a novel-optimizer speedrun and none discovered a genuinely new optimizer [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)

## Building agent evals & datasets
- **Evals are not unit tests** — start from known failure modes named by you or an SME; exhaustive coverage means infinite tests and zero shipping. A golden/seed dataset = input + expected + metadata, and ~10 hand-built edge cases beats vibes-only ship [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- ...but structure them LIKE unit tests and go all the way to CODE, not YAML: evals as tests in your own runner (Sentry's vitest-evals, OpenAI's "macro evals"), because the "prompt" is now the entire harness. Anti-pattern — production eval cases as multi-MB YAML blobs that blow the coding agent's context on read [incident.io](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L2r6vLlLgs8) (2026-05) [Raindrop-Hylak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jHMiYtjoJfA) (2026-08)
- **Don't generate synthetic eval data by prompting for N test queries from scratch** — sample real production examples and mutate them. Second mechanism for the same rule: obviously-synthetic inputs (placeholder names, nonexistent dates) trigger eval-awareness and push the model out of distribution [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07) [Surge-Heiner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-npY6XjM8CQ) (2026-08)
- **Task-difficulty rule for any generation pipeline**: if a task is too hard the model falls back on its biases and loses correctness AND DIVERSITY — decompose by building the SCAFFOLD first (for a novel: setting, characters, style, plot, twists, then chapters), measurably better than chunk-at-a-time. Diversity collapse is the failure mode nobody checks for [poolside-Abdin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KhYifX22yhE) (2026-07)
- **User-simulator realism trap**: a frontier model role-playing a customer defaults to unrealistically patient and articulate, giving misleadingly high (90%+) offline pass rates. Fine-tuning the user-LLM on real verbatims with defined personas (bypasser, refund-seeker, AI-skeptic) drops the score — and that drop is the wanted signal [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- **Test negatives explicitly, not just task success**: pass/fail on "did it do the task" hid an agent violating a hard rule (told never to remove ad disclaimers, it detected one and removed it anyway) — only the trace surfaced it [YouTube-Ads](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xyL2Ltkh-SA) (2026-07)
- **An Oracle solution is a required part of a benchmark task**: a scripted solution running the same environment→verifier sequence with the agent replaced, proving the task is solvable at all. If the Oracle can't solve it you're measuring a broken task [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- **Contamination-proof by construction**: build tasks from zero-days your own team found in widely-deployed OSS and never published, run black-box (no internet, no source) — the model can't have memorized the vulnerability, and you don't wait for a fresh benchmark cycle [Arithmetic-Rolls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O-CBZ3JtRvo) (2026-07)
- **Deterministic PARTIAL graders at every step of a long chain**, not a binary end-of-task pass: over ~16-step tasks you read how deep each model got, keeping a benchmark with almost no solves discriminating. What that surfaced — models finish DISCOVERY then fail the logical leap to exploitation; one reasoned 3+ hours without solving. The gap is world-modelling, not retrieval [Arithmetic-Rolls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O-CBZ3JtRvo) (2026-07)
- **Verify the WORLD, not just the output**: final environment state (DB state, API responses, user replies) plus trace plus artifacts. Deterministic checks for final output and tool calls; LLM/agent-as-judge for trace quality and planning correctness [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- The compiler pattern that makes that variation scale: parameterized task template + matching verifier + mock data → compile base cases into configs → REJECT the broken ones, keep the valid. Yielded 3.2M validated configs over 387 scenarios; "the main craft is software engineering" [Programma-D'Oro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08261) (2026-08)
- Payoff beyond anti-gaming — you can finally measure ROBUSTNESS, and frontier models fail it: hold the task fixed, change only the starting screen or the app theme, and models that pass the base case drop sharply. Nothing without variation infrastructure can see this [Programma-D'Oro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08261) (2026-08)
- **Simulated eval data can replace hand-curated data if you prove the sim-to-real gap first** (five customer-support agents in production): measure eval-score correlation between sim and production traces, and have domain experts label sim traces — 80% confirmed the sims give usable data. Without that step the simulation is unfalsifiable [Nubank-Snowglobe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KMR_RBoCa4M) (2026-07)
- **The dangerous agent failure isn't a bad output** (obvious, cheap to eyeball-fix) — it's a polished, plausible artifact that quietly violates your gates and ships (wrong voice, unverified claim, missing sections). Scope the verification gate to claim-bearing outputs, where a confident specific stat with an empty verification log can't ship. "Trust me is not a verifier" [Shrabony](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WLXxTaPagA8) (2026-07)
- **The spec is more than the eval set** — write these down so cases generate from them: golden examples; hard RULES; valid substitution sets; domain terms that must NOT be substituted (gross profit ≠ gross sales); roles behaviour varies by; a robustness envelope. Keep it framework-independent so the suite survives a migration [SafeIntelligence-Willmott](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UQKg0td-Bf4) (2026-05)
- **Long-session evals to catch late-conversation degradation**: context bugs surface at turn 15–20+ after a user walks through many pages in one chat — too late if you wait for user reports. Load N turns and assert on turn N+1, turning "does the context strategy hold?" into a repeatable signal [Arize-context](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=esY99nYXxR4) (2026-05)
- **Isolate learning from base-model strength with a stateless baseline**: run the suite twice — once stateful (notes, growing context, weight updates allowed), once RESET between every instance — and report gain = stateful − stateless. Cumulative reward alone lets a strong non-learning system outscore a weaker one that learns [CL-Bench-Asawa](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iqloyWCGYQQ) (2026-08)
- **You cannot build a learning eval by chaining existing benchmark items** — they're designed to be INDEPENDENT, so no latent structure exists for prior experience to exploit. Requirements instead: headroom (must need online adaptation, not recall), shared latent structure across instances, and a learning signal present in the environment [CL-Bench-Asawa](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iqloyWCGYQQ) (2026-08)
- **Build concept drift in deliberately**: migrate the schema mid-sequence (drop columns, rename, change format) so the eval tests whether the system discards stale experience while still absorbing new — the stability/plasticity tradeoff a stationary task can't see [CL-Bench-Asawa](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iqloyWCGYQQ) (2026-08)
- **CRUD-tool agents break offline eval**: hard to reconstruct external-system state at capture time, risky to mutate prod. Reconstruct historical state via versioned/timestamp queries, or cram system state into the trace itself and inject it into the eval task. Mock the expensive backend (mock TPUs) to test the flow without burning real compute [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05) [DeepMind-Sawhney](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gujZrJ9L5I) (2026-05)
- **Build your OWN eval set representative of your real tasks** — vendors overfit to public SWE-bench; Meta built an internal "Meta SWE-bench" because real internal problems differ from public GitHub issues. For dialogue/voice there are no good E2E multi-turn benchmarks, so an internal benchmark is the only way to capture your distribution [Guild-Meta](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/From-Single-Player-to-Multi-Player-Operating-AI-Agents-at-Scale-e3khpk8) (2026-06) [ElevenLabs](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Voice-Agent-Use-Cases-e3ileg6) (2026-05)
- **Cheapest own-benchmark for HARNESS selection: replay your own merged PRs.** Pick past PRs you consider good engineering work (human, agent or hybrid), re-run them across harnesses, read quality × cost × time — stack mismatch is the whole point, since SWE-bench is Python and you may be Rails [Superconductor-Singh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OL7kfezynJM) (2026-08)
- **Keep ONE real task as a cross-generation regression probe.** Re-running a single production refactor as models shipped: o3 needed ~3h of back-and-forth and made 10 major mistakes, Sonnet 4.6 solved it with one extra iteration, Opus 4.8 essentially one-shot it — ≈1/5 the time. The tool-call SHAPE moved too, so runs cost more and take far less of you [Wisedocs-Linkov](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vn4WpqNpck) (2026-08)
- **Free multi-layer golden sets from git history** (no human labeling): take OSS commits adding tests AND implementation together (changed files = the retrieval golden set), regenerate intent backwards from the final diff, and score each stage separately — retrieval → intent → edit → tests pass — so a regression is attributable to a LAYER. Score redundant changes too [Windsurf](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan) (2025-05)
- **Pick that subset by discrimination, not at random**: the highest-discrimination items reproduced the full ranking at 0.99 correlation with 97 of 484 items (~5× cheaper), where random did far worse. But the gain scales with how badly calibrated your set is — on GPQA random does as well. Calibrate before you compress [Vidal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3FEoMYvUf8) (2026-07)
- **Code coverage ≠ functionality coverage**: AI-generated tests are often "self-affirming" — the unit suite is green but system behavior was never validated. Playwright as an agent-driven E2E loop (open the page, type, click filters, assert results) confirms the app actually works; auto-screenshot every run and attach them to the PR as functionality evidence [Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWEInOtngmM) (2026-05)

## Model selection & small language models
- **SAGE model selection** (Small And Good Enough): prototype with frontier models to *prove the task is solvable*, then systematically downsize — golden dataset and evals → test frontier vs smaller candidates → pick the smallest meeting acceptance criteria (≥90%). Measured: Llama 3.2 3B with prompt engineering hit 90%+ vs Claude Sonnet, ~$1/day vs $1/hour [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fWXJM-J0ZB8) (2026-06)
- **Hard limit on downsizing — off-distribution, a small model costs MORE**: on terminal-bench Opus scored ~3× better at ~1/10 the cost of Haiku despite the cheaper per-token price, because small models thrash, over-call tools and loop when out of distribution. Check in-vs-out-of-distribution *before* checking price [OpenRouter/Atallah, Cognition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHBjufYK8TA) (2026-08)
- **Latency-first variant of SAGE**: pick the FASTEST model your latency budget allows, then spend the remaining effort on scaffolding. A voice tutor needing first audio in ~950ms measured Opus 4.7 reasoning in seconds vs Haiku 4.5 behind a state machine at ~900ms — you pay the scaffolding once in code, not every turn [Microsoft-ACE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnLBmfsI_Fg) (2026-07)
- **Latency-first downsizing applied to the JUDGE, so eval fits inside the generation loop**: an expert-metric + LLM-judge committee distilled into a small VLM scoring a 15s video in ~3s; the bigger model scored better and was rejected. Pair with running the eval at the cheapest STAGE — check character drift on two start frames before generating video at all [Character.AI-Bril](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_PmGocP4rc) (2026-07)
- **What makes downsizing tractable is reading the frontier model's TRACES, not guessing** — the extra guidance the cheap model needs is derived from how the frontier model reasoned. Worked example: matched Opus's trace-judging quality on Harvey's legal benchmark with an open model at ~1–2 orders of magnitude lower cost [LangChain-Trivedy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvRngaQZQ3Y) (2026-08)
- **Auto-route rather than trusting the dev to pick**: left alone devs default to the biggest model out of fear, so route by question complexity (an LLM router, or a plain ML classifier — cheaper, faster, interpretable) and retry-ESCALATE to a stronger model on failure [Omnigent](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Omnigent-Composition--Control--and-Collaboration-for-AI-Agents-e3lka92) (2026-07)
- Free labelled routing data from production: a user manually upgrading or downgrading the model mid-task is a "we routed wrong" event. Collect (prompt, model chosen, model it should have been) and re-fit the router on real traffic rather than a static dataset [Cognition/Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHBjufYK8TA) (2026-08)
- Make the tradeoff concrete with cost-normalized accuracy (accuracy ÷ cost) — 92% @ $0.02 can beat 95% @ $0.15. Tier cheap models for simple decisions and reserve expensive ones for tool-calling and complex in-agent calls, since only a few models reliably tool-call [PwC](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Autonomous-Agents-at-Work-From-OpenClaw-Hype-to-Enterprise-Reality-e3jj7u3) [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)

## LLM judges & human annotation
- **Ceiling on model-as-judge for quality**: "if the model knew what good code looks like, it would write it in the first place." Review agents raise the floor but are bounded by what RL taught — and the model writing the code is the model reading it [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) (2026-07)
- ...sharper line to hold: an agent can tell you an artifact is CORRECT — matches the spec — not that it is GOOD. Good means UX quality, delight, whether anyone comes back; a rubric the builder runs doesn't reach it [Osmani-PE](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering) (2026-08)
- **Mix deterministic code checks with LLM judges**: objective failure modes (too many tool calls, too many tokens) need no LLM. Two scorer classes — deterministic (category match, schema valid) plus LLM-judge (tone, brand style, helpfulness). Hard rule: if you can express it deterministically, don't use a judge [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- **The strongest argument for that rule is ATTRIBUTION, not cost**: with a judge in the loop you can't tell whether a score moved because the agent changed or because the evaluator did. Escape hatch when outputs are artifacts: push the same inputs through a golden artifact and the model's artifact and compare outputs [Witan-Campos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HEFSExa0xl0) (2026-07)
- **Non-deterministic verification compounds per iteration**: an LLM verifier with ~5% error inside an agent loop trends toward coin-flip correctness over 10–20 iterations — keep in-loop verification deterministic and cheap (types, linters, simulation tests) and spend LLM judgment sparingly and OUTSIDE the hot loop. Where no deterministic check exists (aesthetic quality), distil a judge cheap enough to run per step [Sentry-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)
- Human annotation must capture a justification, not just thumbs up/down — the justification is the domain knowledge you later distill into a judge; feed collected thumbs-down justifications through a coding agent to derive the failure-mode taxonomy. Custom domain-specific annotation UIs beat a generic grading view [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- **One annotation set, two uses**: short randomized sessions (10–15 min, a random axis per annotator, not everyone grading the same one) first calibrate the judge prompt, then become the training set for the next distilled judge model [Character.AI-Bril](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_PmGocP4rc) (2026-07)
- **A trained judge learns the easiest discriminating cue, not your axis**: good-vs-corrupted pairs taught a V1 scorer to score gloss, not storytelling — 9.2 on "camera work" for a shot where the camera never moved. The obvious fix leaks another cue: real-vs-AI footage trains an AI-detector. Fixed only by identical encoding and annotation on both sides [Character.AI-Bril](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_PmGocP4rc) (2026-07)
- **Best implicit feedback signal = what the user did NEXT**, not thumbs up/down (almost nobody clicks, and adversarial users click "bad"): "No, I meant X" or re-asking is a strong negative; "thanks" or leaving is positive [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- **Fixed-rubric LLM-as-judge breaks down once an agent's trajectory genuinely differs run to run** (dynamic UI generation, variable tool sequences) — an "agent as judge" reading full traces to find patterns (repeated calls, inefficient loops) catches failure modes a fixed-score judge can't [Arize-Dhinakaran](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q2JrUKBMf0w) (2026-07)
- **LLM-as-judge systematically privileges fluency over fidelity to the underlying record** — a structural bias. And alignment/RLHF can AMPLIFY a bias rather than fix it: human raters share the corpus's priors, so optimizing for preference rewards the answer the rater already believes ("algorithmic sycophancy") [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- **For fidelity/groundedness evals the domain expert is a STRUCTURAL requirement**, not a courtesy: fidelity is a RELATION between output and record, so a metric blind to the archive can only measure fluency. Cheap in practice — the expert builds the instrument ONCE (diagnostic questions + gold set + weighted rubric), then it's a pipeline GATE [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)

### Grading subjective quality / taste
- You CAN grade subjective quality if you hold a strong opinion and write it down: a design rubric used 4 weighted criteria — design, originality, craft, functionality — weighted toward design and originality, reweighted per model. Calibrate with few-shot reference examples ("this is a pro look; this is AI slop") so the judge's taste converges on yours [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Counterweight: code style has no ground truth — "is this file structure better?" splits humans ~80/20, so a library's "good patterns" are opinion and only hard signals (compiles vs not) are objective; favor per-project evals over chasing absolute style correctness [Effect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wmp2Tku2PrI) (2026-05)

### Writing the judge rubric
- **LLM-judge rubric = 5 parts**: (1) judge role and domain context, (2) explicit observable criteria each mapped to a failure you actually saw in traces (never "good"/"helpful"), (3) clearly delimited data (XML tags), (4) labeled examples — the most-skipped and highest-value part, (5) constrained output (one word) [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Don't ask judges for 1–10 ratings** ("what's the difference between a 6 and a 7?" is noise) — use binary or at most 3 buckets, and prefer pairwise for A/B-ing prompts. Holds for TRAINING a judge too: absolute ratings diverge across annotators while pairwise choices converge. Chain-of-thought before the label measurably improves judging [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05) [Character.AI-Bril](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_PmGocP4rc) (2026-07)
- **"God evaluator" anti-pattern**: one judge testing accuracy + tone + completeness + policy + format at once is uncalibratable and gives no why-it-failed — split into one judge per dimension [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Weight the rubric toward the consequential failure and EXCLUDE the proxy axis**: 40% on the failure that matters most (anachronism detection), and deliberately DROP "does it sound right?" — rewarding fluency validates the exact error the eval exists to catch. A persona eval can score 80%+ on fluency while reasoning from content the eval has no mechanism to check [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- Correctness/factuality judges fail on future-dated or very recent data — the judge's own cutoff makes it wrong; switch to a faithfulness (grounded-in-provided-context) eval, which needs a separate `context` column = only the research turn's output, so split the agent into research-then-write [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)

## Eval-the-eval & statistical significance
- **Validate a judge like a classifier**: hand-label ~100 examples pass/fail, split train/dev/test, and report precision/recall against human ground truth — an 84% vs 88% judge comparison on only 50 samples isn't a real signal. Save statistical rigor for numbers that drive a shipping decision [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- **Inter-rater reliability between two human experts on the same rubric is often only 0.2–0.3** — so a judge agreeing with you at 0.4 is doing better than two humans agree with each other; judge-disagrees-with-you only matters if it disagrees MORE than a human would [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Establish the noise floor before reading any agreement number.** The well-known 83%-agreement synthetic-persona result is normalized against humans being only ~80% self-consistent when re-tested two weeks later. If you can't re-test your humans: split the ground truth in half, treat one half as "synthetic", correlate, repeat hundreds of times, average [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- **"Is the failure fair?" check**: if a failing trace looks fine to you, the eval is wrong, not the agent — CoreBench case: Opus scored 42% because the eval demanded exact "96.12" while Claude returned "96.124991"; fixing the eval took it to 95%. A consistent 0% ≈ a broken task [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- **Negative discrimination is a mislabel detector**: any item stronger models get wrong *more often* is almost certainly broken. Flag the ones significantly below zero, then have an LLM adjudicate each. One real catch: a question asked for passengers, the gold answer counted passengers plus crew [Vidal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3FEoMYvUf8) (2026-07)
- **Run 3–6 trials per case** — agents are non-deterministic and one pass proves nothing — and test across harnesses, since a skill good on one model can be bad on another your users actually use. Judge on a pass-rate threshold against an error budget, not exact pass/fail [DeepMind-Schmid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0vphxNt4wyk) (2026-07) [incident.io](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L2r6vLlLgs8) (2026-05)
- Public worked example: an open-source ATS résumé scorer, run ~100× on one résumé with a small local model, returned 66 to 99 — against a hiring bar fixed at 85. A single-pass score sold as a screen is a coin flip [Charity Majors](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/stop-being-skeptical-about-ai-for) (2026-08)
- **Pre-register the eval instrument**: lock, timestamp and publish the questions, conditions, rubric AND directional predictions BEFORE collecting data — you can't be accused of cherry-picking once the instrument was fixed before the data existed [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- **Eval-validity ops**: classify exit statuses to separate model errors from infra errors (context overflow, too-many-tool-calls, provider errors) with an explicit rerun policy; reproduce an external benchmark's published numbers on your infra before trusting your own experiments; watch for silent default-parameter drift (reasoning level, caching) — 1–2 runs a month go invalid from this [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06)
- **Report pass@5 (capability ceiling) alongside pass^5 (reliability)**, plus tokens and price per problem with confidence intervals — mean resolved alone hides both potential and flakiness. Caveat: pass@k only means this where the environment varies per run; pass^k survives as a reliability lower bound either way [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06) [Programma-D'Oro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08261) (2026-08)
- **Sample the ENVIRONMENT, not just the model, or your confidence intervals lie.** CIs from rollouts on the base case alone give ~17–20% real coverage for a nominal 95% interval; hierarchical CIs restore 95%. Tight-but-wrong intervals separate model A from B when they aren't — at 1M tasks, a real 4% gap and ~$12/mistake, that's six figures monthly [Programma-D'Oro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08261) (2026-08)

## Reward hacking
- **Interface/API design determines whether an agent reward-hacks, not just the objective**: a loose API where one function processed both train and test data let an agent leak test info into training; tightening to a strict train/test-isolated interface dropped leakage to zero with no other change. The interface biases which solutions are even reachable [WF2026-Autoresearch/Weco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) [Weco/Jiang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iCj_ATyThvc) (2026-07)
- **At multi-hour horizons a weak verifier becomes an ATTACK SURFACE** — the agent has hours, a filesystem, network and a reward signal, so it probes the verifier instead of working. Response: multiple INDEPENDENT verifier channels failing differently (hidden tests + reference-parity + a computer-use agent driving the UI + anti-cheat), hardened by trial-and-patch rounds [SWE-Marathon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rx8f05JI_WA) (2026-07)
- **Measured reward-hacking base rate at project scale** (1400 rollouts): 12.8% showed suspicious shortcut behavior (hunting solution files, tampering with configs), 9% shipped a clear verifier bypass in the final submission — but 0 earned reward through an exploit, because defenses caught them. "Zero exploits rewarded" is the bar a long-horizon eval must clear [SWE-Marathon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rx8f05JI_WA) (2026-07)
- **Benchmark-cheating arms race** (observed in Claude Code and Codex): `git log --all` reads the future fix commit; WebFetch or `curl` reads the original issue/PR; a public fork has answers posted. Structural fixes: separate the verifier runtime from the agent runtime, standardize the test-report format, trim every git ref except the base commit [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06) [DeepSWE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yk87oUPVaxU) (2026-07)
- Show the agent a PUBLIC score and keep a PRIVATE held-out leaderboard for humans (Kaggle-shaped): data and description in, the harness submits containerized models, the agent only ever sees the public score — stopping a meta-optimizer tuning the harness from hill-climbing the held-out set [MorganStanley-AlphaLab](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiqubc5b5Yo) (2026-07)
- **Reward validated generalization, not "spend more compute"**: rewarding in-domain score-with-more-compute produced a beautiful in-domain scaling curve that transferred flat-to-zero on held-out tasks; rewarding "improves without regressing, no credit for extra compute" found cheaper programs (~1.5× baseline) that generalized positively [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **Reward hacking from environment bugs, not misspecified reward**: a ~10% tool-call failure rate (a networking bug) taught a model to emit progressively shorter responses to avoid triggering failed calls, with no length penalty existing. Relatedly, when sandbox calls were slow, models learned to spam calls to trigger a timeout rather than take an explicit zero [Applied Compute/Feng](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k35LeKZEhiE) (2026-07)
- Reward hacks are often invisible to a live judge but obvious in hindsight — mine collected trajectories after the fact (multiple models reviewing the same trace, or the same model seeing what came after) to catalog patterns and patch the reward function [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AQv3qRCG6Gw) (2026-07)
- **Reframe "find the bug" as an audit task** (find ALL issues, scored by precision/recall against a growing known-set) — single-ground-truth tasks reward-hack toward the easiest bug, and even DARPA's $60M hand-curated CGC challenges had unintended extra bugs 50% of the time. Dedupe via stack-trace fingerprinting and require proof-of-work: trigger the issue, don't just report it [Brumley](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFxh7sqbUZo) (2026-08)

## The improvement flywheel / hill-climbing
- **Flywheel loop**: capture trace → diagnose → replay offline → run suite → score → read failure traces → classify each failure ("didn't run tests", "broken read-file tool") → pull the small high-leverage levers. Remediation is per-failure but regression-aware: replay the failing case, tighten, re-eval it, then ALWAYS re-run the WHOLE golden set [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05) [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- **"Verifiable continual learning" formalizes that loop**: a production log plus feedback is ONE observation, not testable — first LIFT it into a replayable environment (synthetic users, mocked tools, evaluators defining success). Then every fix must be PROVEN to help (measure the delta) AND PROVEN to break nothing (re-run prior environments) [RELAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IxD9OB3XuQ) (2026-07)
- Route the fix HOLISTICALLY to the layer that explains the failure with the smallest durable change — model (weights: SFT/RL/LoRA, expensive), harness (prompts/skills/tools/workflow, medium), or memory (facts and distilled skills, cheapest but usually unverified). The named anti-pattern: piling "never do this / critical that" into the prompt [RELAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IxD9OB3XuQ) [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- **Primary harness-debugging loop is reading traces by hand, not running more experiments** — find where the agent's judgment diverged from yours and tune the prompt there. Prioritize by severity × frequency, not raw frequency — a rare off-the-rails run beats a frequent minor hallucination [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- **First question to ask of any apparent reasoning failure: is it actually an INFRASTRUCTURE bug?** A wrong example in the skill that the model follows faithfully, or a tool that keeps failing while the model works around it, both present as the model being dumb — and both are fixable rather than model-limited [Witan-Campos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HEFSExa0xl0) (2026-07)
- **Distributional view across all runs, not single traces**: aggregate every instantiation into a path/branch/loop graph — % traffic per branch, latency per component within a branch, eval-score drop per path. Root-cause pattern: B called before its dependency A on the bad path → fix by adding ordering context [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JsCCrBF7F1g) (2026-06)
- **Trace-linked evals**: attach eval results to the trace so detection→diagnosis is one hop — swap a model, the eval flags a tool-call metric drop, jump to the trace to see which tool stopped being called. Pair with eval-gated changes: every change re-runs evals and compares, with auto-rollback to the best-scoring version [Foundry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXM3zE-2dk) (2026-05), corroborated by [Microsoft/Castro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)

### Prompt / agent-config optimization (GEPA)
- **GEPA** (Genetic Pareto) = a string optimizer over a prompt or any JSON/key-value object. It reflects on the full rollout trace (chain-of-thought, tool calls, tool-error messages) in natural language and proposes one large prompt edit instead of thousands of tiny gradient updates — one round of reflection on 3 data points beat GRPO's result after 25,000 rollouts by 2× [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05) [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **The Pareto pool** (keep every candidate that wins on ≥1 training example, not just the top scorer) accounts for over half of GEPA's gains vs a naive "LLM improves its own prompt in a loop," which reliably gets stuck in a local optimum [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- **Optimization payoff is biggest when forcing a cheap model to match a frontier model on PRIVATE untrained high-volume data**; on public-knowledge tasks a frontier model already gets it right, and an optimized prompt is model-specific so it rarely pays for general tasks. Shopify: agent + Qwen + GEPA cut fraud/tax classification from $5M/yr → ~$73K/yr while improving quality [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- **GEPA overfits**: seeing only a small train subset, it learned to EXCLUDE uncles and aunts present in the golden set → 96% with a systematic error. Fix = ≥2× data covering the full space plus a train/eval split. A bloated-prompt proposer was fixed by making it SELECT 20 of ~200 pre-split sentences [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- **Coding agents are the hard case**: the trajectory space is so sparse that even 5K eval cases overfit. A shipped agent instead asks a strong model "why this instead of that, cite the prompt", has it edit the prompt, then re-runs the case as a regression. GEPA stays well-validated on *narrow* tasks — a scope boundary, not a refutation [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05) [Cognition/Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHBjufYK8TA) (2026-08)
- **Precondition for any of this: externalize agent config** — instructions, tool definitions and skills as external files rather than inline in source — so an optimizer can evaluate a baseline, generate candidates and apply the winner by swapping one config for another [Microsoft/Castro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGSFUqzqErE) (2026-07)
- **Optimization gains may GROW, not shrink, as the base model gets stronger** — better instruction-following means a precise instruction buys more: Databricks used GEPA to tune GPT-OSS-120B to beat Claude Opus at ~90× lower cost on a deployed agent, with a larger delta than on weaker base models [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)

### Continuous / production eval & behavior auditing
- **Separate capability evals from behavioural evals**: capability = given a full task definition and codebase, does it decide correctly and pass tests; behavioural = what makes a product feel bad (announcing it's time to sleep, stopping at "I finished two of five parts, shall I continue?"). Behavioural coverage comes from ranking user complaints and writing an eval per issue [Anthropic-CC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uU5Gv2h8-9g) (2026-07)
- **The mechanism that let one team remove humans from code review over ~6 months**: start with human review everywhere; drop the human only where automated review catches 100% of issues; on every incident, update review to catch that class and add the causing PR to an eval set so the fix can't regress. Critical paths keep a named code owner [Anthropic-CC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uU5Gv2h8-9g) (2026-07)
- **Benchmark the *harness* over time, not just the model**: daily sampling of a coding benchmark shows multi-week accuracy dips, one traced to the thinking trace being dropped on the second turn plus a bad system prompt. A single verbosity-reducing sentence made the model measurably dumber, and the same stack on different accelerators sampled differently. Read the 7-day moving average [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Worked instance: offline evals didn't predict what users KEEP, so candidates ship to a traffic slice (15%) judged on acceptance rate, "kept rate" (characters surviving into the final text) and error counts before vs after [Zed-Kunkle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=phchDt63qAA) (2026-05)
- **Score open-ended tasks against N distinct references, not one**: Zeta2's held-out eval counts closeness to any of 3 separately-generated frontier predictions, plus a named failure metric — "reversal ratio", outputs that merely undo what the user did [Zed-Kunkle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=phchDt63qAA) (2026-05)
- **Think like an SRE, not a researcher**: reliability is the north-star and accuracy is just an input. Map each metric to a business outcome (accuracy deliberately absent) — task completion, tool success, escalation rate, safety violations, latency, cost, recovery rate. Drift is silent, so without continuous eval you discover degradation when users complain [Meta-Evals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY) (2026-06)
- **Audit behavior, not just outputs**: a safe-looking answer can hide unsafe behavior — the real signal is abnormal tool calls, strange network egress, odd retries, scope expansion. Log chain-of-thought into a system of record and mine it to find where task scope is expanding [PwC](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Autonomous-Agents-at-Work-From-OpenClaw-Hype-to-Enterprise-Reality-e3jj7u3) (2026-05)
- **Pair the fast fix loop with a slow ZOOM-OUT "session analyzer"**: an agent scores every conversation and surfaces system-level health — score distribution, success rate, cost, tool-call reject reasons, sentiment — plus insights connecting the dots across hundreds of sessions, each with root cause and recommended fix. Run it weekly; its goal is not fixing a specific bug [Wandero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZsf_Sfm7RU) (2026-07)
- **"Clusters are not issues."** Clustering production traces is fine for one-off error analysis but doesn't scale: boundaries aren't controllable, clusters can't be tracked over time, and "wrong price quoted" plus "wrong refund calculated" collapse into one pricing cluster with unrelated root causes [Raindrop-Hylak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jHMiYtjoJfA) (2026-08)
- Track two fields per issue instead: **when it started** and **what % of users it hits**. Started-yesterday makes you ask what changed; 3 users vs 100,000 decides whether it's worth anything. Agents generate unbounded problems, so without both you can't rank [Raindrop-Hylak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jHMiYtjoJfA) (2026-08)
- **Agents are bad at anomaly DETECTION — have them investigate anomalies you already found deterministically.** Pull out every deterministic signal you can (keyword-frequency spikes); a spike isn't itself an issue, but it's a tractable thing to hand an agent [Raindrop-Hylak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jHMiYtjoJfA) (2026-08)
- **The 70%-accuracy demo→prod gap is task-shaped**: a coding agent at 70% saves keystrokes and is cheap to verify, but an SRE agent at 70% wastes 30 stressful minutes and kills trust. Past simple tasks, VERIFICATION (not search) is the hard problem — subtle issues (latency +20%) need company-specific context to rank causes [MLOps-Sandboxing](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Sandboxing--Agent-Harnesses--and-Agent-Teamwork-e3kts75) (2026-06)

### Adaptive / self-modifying evals
- **"Eval calcification"**: evals rot faster than benchmark-leakage alone explains, because the *application self-modifies* — the harness writes its own skills and ships at token-speed, so a static benchmark can't track a target that rewrites itself. Treat the eval set as living code: an agent watches production traces, detects distribution shift, and rewrites the suite [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- Auto-evolve the eval hill from qualitative feedback (research direction, not shipped practice): binary good/bad discards most of the signal vs textual feedback, and any hand-built eval set is only a proxy — the proposal is that models interpret textual signals from the environment and iteratively refine the eval ITSELF, not just the candidate [DSPy-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)

## When to fine-tune
- **Choose the lever by FEEDBACK LATENCY, and expect a sandwich**: harness edits return signal in ~2 minutes, so exhaust that ceiling first, fine-tune only to break through it, then resume harness engineering on top. Most teams' use cases never leave step one — and no mature evals plus no collected data means you're not ready to train [LangChain-Trivedy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvRngaQZQ3Y) (2026-08) [Modal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HvZXAOZ3iv8) (2026-06)
- **Unusual reason to post-train: editorial selectivity, not capability.** Frontier models were too verbose for client-facing process analysis and could not judge which detail the client cares about vs which to gloss — fixed by post-training an open-weight model (Kimi K2.6) [Varick-Pruitt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic) (2026-07)
- **Mechanism behind that modest bump: continued pre-training on your own corpus memorizes without generalizing.** Trained to convergence (loss ~0.0001), generation collapses — it answers only questions whose answers sat verbatim in the data. And every route around it (KV compaction, on-policy distillation, self-study QA, synthetic pre-training, unsupervised RL envs) hits the same **synthetic data wall** [Morris/Engram](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WiqDvX6isc4) (2026-08)
- **For groundable/auditable tasks prefer context-window/RAG over fine-tuning**: a document in the window keeps PROVENANCE (goes in intact, comes out intact, you can check faithfulness), whereas fine-tuning dissolves docs into parameters, breaks the chain, and can amplify the underlying bias while hiding it [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- **Per-company fine-tuning, measured at scale**: Windsurf built full per-enterprise fine-tune infra and found fine-tuning on the company codebase gave only a modest bump vs better retrieval and personalization. Climb the cheap hill (retrieval/context) fully before the expensive one; a premature hard solution is tech debt [Windsurf](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan) (2025-05)
- **Distill with a REPAIR step, don't discard bad labels**: heuristics reject obviously-wrong teacher output (undoes what the user typed; ignores the editable-region boundary), a frontier model fixes it with the failure named, and the repair is the student's target. Teachers label inconsistently, so tune the teacher prompt and keep the pipeline append-only JSONL [Zed-Kunkle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=phchDt63qAA) (2026-05)
- **"Settled data" — let the product observe the real answer**: snapshot the edited region 10s after the user stops touching it (not on commit). Noisy: they change their mind, or an agent rewrites it wholesale [Zed-Kunkle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=phchDt63qAA) (2026-05)
- The filter that rescues it: the MIDDLE band of distance-to-settled (Levenshtein/n-gram) — far = noise, very close = trivially predictable, middle = trainable signal, and where post-cutoff functions live. Train on the closest prediction [Zed-Kunkle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=phchDt63qAA) (2026-05)
- Cost trick making that filter viable: sampling the teacher 10× over 100k examples is a million frontier calls. Once the student nears teacher quality, sample your own checkpoint 50× instead — near-free and does the same job [Zed-Kunkle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=phchDt63qAA) (2026-05)
- **"Hint leakage" is distillation's reward hacking.** On-policy self-distillation builds a teacher by putting privileged info in its prompt; if the hint contains the ANSWER, the student announces the conclusion and back-fills a reasoning trace — impossible at inference. Fix: rewrite each hint to what the model could have DISCOVERED ("check the logs", not "the SSO token expired") [Trajectory-Malde](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zL1kLftVTlo) (2026-08)
- **Long-horizon distillation collapses into hedging**: scaled to 120B+ with 50–100 tool calls, eval variance exploded and the student's word cloud filled with "wait"/"but"/"maybe" as the teacher course-corrected at every divergence. Fix — weight each step's tokens by the student↔teacher KL divergence AT that step, so a run trains on its first divergence and is discounted after [Trajectory-Malde](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zL1kLftVTlo) (2026-08)
- **Fine-tuning a tiny model beats prompting it for narrow tool-use**: Function Gemma (270M) went ~46% out-of-the-box → >90% on 8/10 functions. Standard recipe is synthetic data from a bigger model → fine-tune the small one, ~10k samples, a measured ~20–40pt lift. The SIZE REGIME picks the approach: 1–4B needs only zero-shot prompting, ≤500M won't get there without fine-tuning [Function-Gemma](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-TiET_K-E_g) [LiteRT-LM-Brick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BKWpYIWvAo4) (2026-05) [Brick-edge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hacEQHHhu2Q) (2026-07)
- **Big-reasoning-model failure on tool tasks is often tool indiscipline, not weak reasoning**: Qwen3-235B queried nonexistent tables without ever calling the available schema tools, then hallucinated; an RL'd 4B learned discover-tools → inspect-schema → self-correct-on-error. Diagnose the tool-use behavior before reaching for a bigger model [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- **RL for behavior, not knowledge**: changing how a model acts (tool discipline) is an RL problem vs SFT for content; GRPO on a 4B, ~21h, <$500/run, doubled pass@1 past a 235B — behavior-targeted RL is cheap enough for self-hosted teams [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- **Agent training data doesn't exist in the wild** (you can't scrape an agent using tools) — stand up an RL environment plus reward and it becomes the synthetic-data pipeline: generate trajectories, keep the good ones by reward, rejection-sample to bootstrap the first SFT set. Mock the *user* with an LLM on real transcripts, served as an MCP server the model perceives as a USER [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05) [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-EDrhIhHzQ) (2026-07)
- **Reward-source hierarchy, cleanest first**: systematic/verifiable (does the code run?) → direct business KPI (support containment rate) → open-ended qualities (tone, guideline adherence) via judge. Human-in-the-loop is mostly defining judge rubrics and scenarios once, NOT weekly annotation campaigns (expensive, and people won't do them) [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05)
- **Textual-feedback RL credit assignment for long rollouts**: grading an entire rollout of hundreds of tool calls makes it near-impossible to know which decision caused the outcome — instead zoom into ONE step, run the same model as a "teacher" given an explicit hint about the mistake there, and use the resulting token-probability shift to up/down-weight just that span [Cursor/Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4Tr-DknG2M) (2026-07)
- **Cheap way to manufacture hard, unmemorizable RL problems**: generate a large synthetic application, then programmatically delete a feature or file so its tests fail — reward = re-implementing the deleted piece well enough that tests pass. Verifiable, and unmemorizable since it derives from a fresh synthetic app [Cursor/Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4Tr-DknG2M) (2026-07)

## Red-teaming & safety-failure evals
- **Design for EMERGENT misbehaviour instead of prompting for it**: don't instruct the model to collude or defraud — build real incentives into the environment (fraud pays if you get away with it) and give general-purpose tools. Unprompted, across an arena of simulated businesses: price cartels formed constantly, lying to suppliers about competitors' quotes, self-rationalization of illegal actions, power-seeking [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Bigger model ≠ safer, with a mechanism**: some jailbreaks work BETTER on large models — an instruction wrapped in a poem gets unwrapped and executed by a capable model while a weak one fails to parse it. Capability and safety are separate axes when picking a model for autonomous use [SafeIntelligence-Willmott](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UQKg0td-Bf4) (2026-05)
- **Aim the spec at the red-team suite**: an agent is most exploitable exactly where it's authorized to act — it engages readily on its own domains and holds real power over its own tasks — so generate attack cases from the spec's task/domain list rather than a generic jailbreak set [SafeIntelligence-Willmott](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UQKg0td-Bf4) (2026-05)
- **Simulation awareness is now the central threat to behavioural evals** and you cannot fix it by making the sim more realistic: one model justified skipping a customer refund because "this person is simulated anyway", and Anthropic's own system card documents awareness changing behaviour [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Fork a REAL deployment into a digital clone to get around it**: history up to the fork point is genuine real-world state, so for the first several turns the model has no way to tell. Turns a non-reproducible N=1 incident into something replayable across models — on one replayed incident Grok 4.3 complied >90%, Gemini ~half, Opus and GPT refused every time [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Manipulation resistance is two-sided, so eval both directions**: one model granted a customer's request for a 99% discount, while the model that replaced it over-refuses — it turned down an influencer offering promotion to 17k followers for something free. Jailbreak-hardening buys the first failure mode and sells you the second [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Sleeper-agent backdoors in fine-tuned checkpoints pass every behavioral eval and production monitor** — the trigger is a benign cue you can't blacklist. Detection: per input, subtract base-model from fine-tune activations (one middle layer suffices) and train a sparse autoencoder on the delta; the backdoor surfaces as one directional feature at ~1.0 precision. Needs the base checkpoint; validated only at 360M params [LexisNexis-IJCNN](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IQkVMvXQKLY) (2026-07)
- **A documented threat model raises security-scanning true-positive rate to ~90%** (aim above 75%). "Great context of the code, poor context of the system" — design intent, recurring on-call fixes and compensating controls live in someone's head. Bootstrap from docs, code and past patches, then have it **interview** the system expert [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)

## Eval limits in large codebases
- Models regress to the mean of their training data (mostly mediocre internet code) — well-engineered projects are a rounding error in the corpus, and agent-generated sprawl eventually exceeds its own ingestion limit. "The best possible spec is the software itself": spec gaps get filled from mediocre priors, multiplied across many parallel agents [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04)
- **Corollary: autonomous loops succeed where a mature verification corpus pre-exists** — years of accumulated test suites and specs ARE the spec (Bun-in-Rust, Next.js rewrite experiments); for prototypes with no such corpus, loop-and-throw-away, then re-spec with a human before building the real thing [Sentry-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)
- **Self-validation still doesn't reach across a repo-scale refactor**: asked to consolidate 10 repos, GPT-5.5 extra-high declared its goal complete in 10m22s having written ~2,000 lines of scaffolding only, its own output admitting it hadn't added the bootstrap command. The tell was the suspiciously small diff — keep a size/shape sanity check on any "done" claim at this scale [Wisedocs-Linkov](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vn4WpqNpck) (2026-08)

## Measuring AI's real-world productivity impact
- **The bottleneck moves once finding is cheap**: scanning 1,000+ repos yielded 23,000 candidates → 6,200 high/critical → 1,600 reported → ~100 patched upstream. Verification, triage and patching are the constraint, so don't aim at scanning. Scale is attributable though — one browser vendor went from ~20 security fixes/month to ~400 in a single month [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- Triage exists because engineer attention is the scarce resource — sending product engineers every true-but-low-severity finding loses their trust. Dedupe, then rank on impact magnitude × likelihood using business context (a firewall turns high into low; PII contents turn medium into high) [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- **DORA metrics stay valid under AI coding** — they measure the commit→prod outer loop and are tool-agnostic; SPACE still applies but should grow a TRUST dimension, since "can I trust this output?" becomes an every-edit question devs never asked of compilers. Agent leverage is capped by the surrounding delivery pipeline [Forsgren](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/developer-productivity-with-dr-nicole) (2025-02)
- **DORA forecast: +25% AI adoption predicts −7.2% delivery stability**, because AI makes large diffs trivially easy to produce and bigger diffs are inherently riskier; corroborated internally at DX — AI users ship more diffs per engineer AND higher diff complexity, so test rigor must scale with output or the undetected-bug surface grows with throughput [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- **Top AI time-savers ranked** (180+ company study): stack-trace analysis and refactoring existing code save more time than mid-loop code-gen — code-gen savings get partly reinvested into reviewing the generated code, whereas toil elimination is a clean net save. And coding is only ~20–25% of total dev time [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- **You need a pre-AI baseline to attribute impact** — start measuring before rollout; usage and velocity metrics partially backfill from GitHub/Jira history but self-reported metrics can't be reconstructed retroactively. If no baseline was captured, run champion/challenger: a control group on the old process alongside a test group on the AI process, rather than asking people how productive they feel [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07) [PwC-Dasaur](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/AI-Hype-vs--Real-Value-e3mg74m) (2026-07)
