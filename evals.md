# Evals

## Reading benchmark numbers
- The harness decides the score: the same benchmark measured ~40% via one CLI agent vs ~50% via a controlled harness, and ~20% vs ~40% for another. Cross-model comparisons need one controlled environment or the numbers are meaningless. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Inference providers are "throughput maxing, accuracy minimizing": across providers serving the *same* open-weight model, a ~10-point spread was measured (76.4% vs 62.4%). Check your provider's accuracy before adopting — open weights get blamed for what the provider did. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Benchmark vendors disagree about each other by an order of magnitude — one reports its own false-positive rate at 0.3% while a competitor measures it at 44.9%. Treat single-benchmark claims accordingly; average across several, noting that the weighting is then the unsolved problem. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Benchmark bugs are mundane and huge: a maths benchmark correction moved a frontier model from ~50% to ~80%, caused by answer-extraction errors (dropped minus signs, off-by-ones). Fixing extraction alone raised one open model from 10% to 25%, and tokenization choices shift MMLU ~0.4%. Low open-weight scores are often formatting, not capability. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Two criteria for a benchmark worth trusting: hard to bench-max (effectively infinite sampling space) *and* cheaply verifiable without an LLM judge — e.g. generated arithmetic, or "a 70-word poem containing 'happy'" where you count and grep. Nobody has built this well yet. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Distrust model-lab benchmark numbers — benchmark-maxing is rampant; confirm on real-world use. [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- **"Don't let the test writer grade the task."** The dominant contrived-data recipe: hire domain experts, have them chat-generate plausible tasks, solve them, cherry-pick the cases where the model diverged, package as a hard north-star benchmark — then sell the training data that hill-climbs that same benchmark. Goodhart with a profit motive. Practical rule: stop outsourcing your definition of realism to the vendors you buy your evals and tasks from. [State of Data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyIoTOAbRfs) (2026-07)
- Structural tell for a contrived benchmark: it only ever tests a single isolated in-distribution question, because the tasks were authored to be solvable in isolation — so it structurally cannot test whether a model sustains correct reasoning across a long dependent episode. [State of Data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyIoTOAbRfs) (2026-07)
- Sharper framing of the harness-decides-the-score finding above: a single benchmark number under a single scaffold is **one sample from a distribution nobody measured** — "like using the screwdriver bit of a Swiss army knife to cut cheese and concluding the knife is broken." Cross-harness and cross-infrastructure differencing is named as the primary cause of benchmark divergence, with high false-positive AND false-negative rates in the SWE-shaped benchmarks (incl. DeepSWE) invisible in headline stats. [State of Data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyIoTOAbRfs) (2026-07)
- Distrust vendor PRODUCTIVITY figures the same way: the famous Copilot "55% faster" number was a single controlled with-vs-without task case study, never intended as a general productivity metric (per GitHub's own then-CEO) — don't cite one-task deltas as org-wide gains. [Dohmke-GitHub](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/github) (2025-06)
- Lab-published numbers aren't comparable across labs: each tunes orchestration to its own model (e.g. compaction via its own API), so the same benchmark yields different results depending on who ran it. [DeepMind-Kang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ubwb6NzegyA) (2026-05)
- Evals rot: SWE-bench verified no longer measures frontier coding (per OpenAI). Hunt new, precise evals. [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- **Evals written today obsolete in ~3 months**: every 3 months a model gets 20% better, so evals saturate unless they're on extremely hard problems; people used to say "this will never be solved by AI, fundamentally distinguishes humans," then models pass the goalposts; if you can move the goalpost, models will pass it. Most practical takeaway: don't waste time writing evals for basic model IQ/reasoning alone — ride the model-improvement wave. [CodeGen/ClickUp](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Coding-Agents-Are-Secretly-General-Agents-e3lad79) (2026-06)
- Time-splits are the only real benchmark decontamination: released questions+solutions leak into next-gen pretraining — collect only fresh tasks from the previous month and re-evaluate monthly. [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06)
- Public benchmarks (terminal-bench v1, SWE-bench variants) contain never-completable tasks forming a noise floor that masks whether models actually improved — saturation/failure-rate trends partly measure task quality, not model progress. [Snorkel-tasks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYH0DMQr30A) (2026-06)
- Static benchmarks go stale fast (authors move on); PvP/ELO (Bradley-Terry) benchmarks resist saturation — always a winner and loser, so they stay hill-climbable. [DeepMind-Kang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ubwb6NzegyA) (2026-05)
- Model endpoints don't always reveal which model actually served a request — undermines reproducibility and over-time comparisons. [DeepMind-Kang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ubwb6NzegyA) (2026-05)
- Adoption cadence: stay current but not earliest adopter — let a new model "set on fire" ~2 weeks before switching. [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- ...but once you've tuned prompts/instructions to a specific model, treat a major version bump like an on-prem upgrade you can defer — a new model can break a setup that worked, so pin the version for tuned/production agents and re-validate before cutting over ("if it ain't broke" applies to LLMs). Reconciles with the cadence above: explore new models promptly, but gate the production cutover on a re-run of your evals. [CI/CD Erez](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/cicd-with-robert-erez) (2026-06)
- Benchmark tool-catalog size before assuming schema bloat is free — accuracy collapses hard as the catalog grows, and semantic routing holds flat; numbers and mitigations live in harnesses.md § Tool design for agents. [Prosodica](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vh2VGuQ3zhY) (2026-06)
- Beyond leaderboard score, measure an auto-research agent's community impact via PR-citation H-index (how much its accepted work gets reused/cited by others): an agent that ran unattended 22 days and set 7 leaderboard records used a 28% submission-to-leaderboard hit rate (~6x the community average) as the real edge over raw throughput, and its H-index (10) beat the top human's (7). [Weco/Aiden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)

## What benchmarks miss (quality, security, the harness)
- An eval harness config-driven in YAML lets non-engineers (analysts, domain experts) contribute test cases alongside engineers — parallelized for throughput across thousands of cases, runnable identically at dev time, pre-commit, and in CI as a regression gate. [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- Maintainability is a *training* gap, not a harness gap: SWE-bench-shaped benchmarks give binary reward on "tests pass, nothing else broke", so nothing penalises poor program design — hence try/catch and casts added purely to go green. [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) (2026-07)
- Why it resists training: the cost function of bad architecture is measured in months, so the reward signal can't propagate back across the gap. Verifying maintainability is orders of magnitude harder than "it runs and tests pass". [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) (2026-07)
- Benchmarks that call an LLM to verify the answer are unsound, and a widely-cited coding benchmark does exactly that: 8.5% false-positive, 24% false-negative. The obvious questions have no good answer — which model verifies, how many samples, and what happens when the verifier has a bad week. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Worse, that benchmark hands the model the full git history — i.e. the answer. Measured cheating differs by model family, though it's arguably the designed behaviour if you supplied the solution. Weak maintainer-written tests are a second false-positive source. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- Maintainability-benchmark frontier worth tracking: ~400-hour multi-feature tasks, uncontaminated task sets built from problems never solved in the real world, and multi-PR benchmarks that penalise tests which don't fail pre-patch plus a code-quality judge. [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) (2026-07)
- Standard coding benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, SWE-bench) measure only functional pass rate — blind to security, maintainability, cyclomatic/cognitive complexity, tech debt; high pass rate ≠ shippable code. Pass rate is empirically uncorrelated with code quality: top-accuracy models still emit hundreds of security issues and bugs per million LOC. [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NuePCNMpWGc) (2026-05)
- BaxBench (baxbench.com, ETH Zurich + UC Berkeley): a named security-specific codegen eval to run alongside functional pass rate — even the best frontier models introduce security vulnerabilities ~20–40% of the time when writing backend code. [Cable/Corridor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JgIS42mz7U) (2026-07)
- Verbosity is exploding across generations — same task set yields <250k LOC on older models vs ~1M LOC on newer; track LOC-per-task as a quality signal. [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NuePCNMpWGc) (2026-05)
- As models mature via RL they fix obvious bug classes but introduce finer, subtler bugs harder for humans to catch — newer ≠ safer, just differently risky. [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NuePCNMpWGc) (2026-05)
- Deterministic code-review eval (Sonar CCREP / c-CRAB): turn each historical human review comment into ONE executable test (passes iff the suggested fix was applied), then show the AI reviewer the PR → have a coding agent apply its comments → run the tests → count human findings recovered. Avoids brittle token-similarity / LLM-as-judge comparison. Result: all current AI review tools COMBINED recovered only 41.5% of human findings; AI is strong on robustness/testing/edge-cases, humans strong on maintainability/design (more codebase context) — use AI review as the first layer, route weak categories to humans. [Sonar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xQnSNlBP_w) (2026-05)
- Empirical shipped-code quality differs by bot, not just by benchmark (Greptile 5M-PR study; ~27.6% of April PRs fully vibe-coded and climbing): no agent wins all metrics, but Claude + Codex had LOWER revert rates than the human baseline, and the AI revert advantage GROWS with PR size (more files changed). Bug SHAPE is bot-specific — Cursor background agents over-index on N+1 query bugs, Claude on missing-tenant-check/auth bugs, Codex on config — so tune review per bot's failure shape; your validation must scale with AI output. Detect vibe-coded PRs via 3 signals: GitHub co-author field (~1%, sparse), PR-description bot notes (~20%), branch-name prefixes (`codex/...`). [Greptile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xQnSNlBP_w) (2026-05)
- Enterprise is the hardest eval (Swix): SWE-bench is one number, but the real eval surface is multi-dimensional — Cognition runs many internal evals each tied to a $10–100M use case spanning multiple orgs/Slacks/IT systems; "show me an RL environment harder than a real enterprise." [Cognition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xQnSNlBP_w) (2026-05)
- Coding benchmarks capture a fraction of real environment complexity — org-specific policies, Slack context, screenshots, flaky toolchains, distributed CI, reviewer-head knowledge, parallel contributors — and that gap is where agents fail in production; discount scores accordingly. [Snorkel-bench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iNkFlCiij0U) (2026-06)
- An eval simultaneously tests three things — the model, the harness, and whether the problems are real; a strong model masks a weak harness, and ambiguity over what's under test is the core agentic-eval problem. Harness dominates coding-eval results: SWE-Bench Pro showed ~22% swing from harness alone while frontier models sat within a few points (same model varies by harness, e.g. Claude Code vs others) — the harness must actually leverage the model, or you're measuring the harness, not the model. [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06) [DeepMind-Kang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ubwb6NzegyA) (2026-05)
- First-hand scaffold-gap numbers at project scale (SWE-Marathon: 20 project-scale tasks — build a Slack clone, a C compiler in Rust; avg trial 31M tokens, longest 877M): best config (Claude Opus 4.8 + Claude Code) resolves only 26%, GPT-5.5 + Codex 12% at far lower cost — a >2x resolution gap attributable to the scaffold (planning, tool use, context summarization, when-to-test), not just the model; failures are deep, not shallow. [SWE-Marathon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rx8f05JI_WA) (2026-07)
- Information retrieval (does the agent find ALL relevant code?) is unsolved — unseen code is where garbage comes from; benchmarks rarely test this. [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04)
- Oracle-gap method to attribute agent failure to retrieval vs reasoning: run the model with the gold documents in context (oracle ceiling) vs with its default tools over the noisy corpus — on BrowseComp-Plus and a financial-docs QA benchmark default agent tools land well below the oracle while swapping in a stronger search tool recovers most of the gap, i.e. the bottleneck is knowledge access, not reasoning. Cheap diagnostic before blaming the model. [Mixedbread](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1IdzkRVmWAA) (2026-07)
- Recursive/RLM harnesses are "too hot to benchmark" — another instance of harness contaminating model scores: a small model in an RLM harness (model-chosen decomposition + tool-calling over the prompt) jumps results dramatically (Symbolica's Agentica reported ~30%+ on ARC-AGI-3 within hours of release vs ~2-3% for frontier models as plain LLMs), so leaderboards either refuse them or split off a separate open-harness leaderboard to keep the no-tool-calling intent clean. [OpenProse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3hXJI2q0Jz8) (2026-06)
- RL reward on SWE-bench-style coding benchmarks is binary pass/fail ("tests pass without breaking others") — nothing penalizes ugly workarounds (needless try/catch nesting, casting just to satisfy a type checker); the cost of bad architecture surfaces months later, too late to attribute reward back to the training episode, so labs structurally cannot yet train "stays maintainable" the way they train "tests pass." Explains why one-shot task performance keeps climbing while maintenance quality doesn't. [HumanLayer/Dex Horthy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- Emerging maintainability-focused RL benchmarks trying to make "stayed maintainable" trainable, beyond binary pass/fail: SWE Marathon (Abundant AI, ~400-hour tasks e.g. "clone all of Excel feature-by-feature"), DeepSWE (Data Curve, large OSS repos deliberately excluded from training data so they can't be memorized), Frontier Code (Cognition, multi-PR tasks that penalize tests not actually failing on the pre-patch code, plus a judge model scoring code-quality-rule adherence). [HumanLayer/Dex Horthy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- Auto-research agents combine, they don't (yet) originate: almost all of one agent's competition-winning ideas traced back to humans (papers, other competitors' abandoned notes) — its edge was finding/combining/reliably implementing existing ideas, not inventing new mechanisms; separately, Claude/Codex/Kimi ran 5+ days on a novel-optimizer speedrun and none discovered a genuinely new optimizer, only recombinations of published methods. A high benchmark/leaderboard score from an auto-research agent doesn't mean it's generating novel ideas. [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- Clean vs. messy repos show roughly the SAME agent task pass rate — clean code's payoff is fewer tokens spent and fewer re-visits/revisions per task, not higher success rate; maintainability is a cost/time lever for agentic coding, not a pass/fail one. [Sonar via WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07) (low-confidence src, third-hand-cited survey)

## Building agent evals & datasets
- User-simulator realism trap: a frontier model role-playing a customer defaults to unrealistically patient and articulate, giving misleadingly high (90%+) offline pass rates. Fine-tuning the user-LLM on real user verbatims (with defined personas — bypasser, refund-seeker, AI-skeptic) drops the score, and that drop is the correct, wanted signal — an eval too easy tells you nothing about production readiness. [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- Don't generate synthetic eval data by prompting an LLM for N test queries from scratch — sample real production examples directly and mutate them to cover golden paths and edge cases. [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- Frame every LLM-judge eval around task success/failure as a binary outcome, built with domain experts so metrics tie to actual business outcomes rather than generic scores ("response helpfulness: 0.5" gives no actionable next step). [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- Validate a judge like a classifier: hand-label ~100 examples pass/fail, split train/dev/test, report precision/recall against human ground truth — an 84% vs 88% judge comparison on only 50 samples isn't a real signal; save statistical rigor (confidence intervals, careful sampling) for numbers that will actually drive a shipping decision. [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- Criteria drift is real and expected: your definition of "good" evolves as you look at more data, so the judge and its criteria should co-develop with ongoing model observation rather than being frozen upfront. [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- Continuous error-analysis loop, run weekly/biweekly not as a one-off audit: deep-dive raw traces → pinpoint failure modes → keep only metrics that actually change a decision → re-evaluate → repeat. Feeds back into three levers — model learning (weights), context learning (what the agent sees), harness learning (prompt/schema/control-flow). [Lyft-Ung](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y) (2026-07)
- Agent development is closer to ML than to software engineering, because you no longer know what the code will do before running it — treat agent performance itself as a black-box artifact managed by empirical evaluation. [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- The mapping that tells you which tools you're missing: training data → environments; test set → evals (also environments); weights → skills/prompts/tools/model; loss → environment rewards; optimizer → text-based optimisation or a coding agent in a loop; gradient step → a pull request; overfitting → reward hacking. [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- An environment is four things: an instruction, a sandbox, a stopping condition, and a verifier (tests, rubrics, or an inspecting agent). Specifying it as a directory layout has become near-standard, which makes environments interoperable between teams. [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- Rollout anatomy: start sandbox → hand to agent (inside or driving it) → run to a stopping condition producing a trajectory → hand sandbox to verifier → stop, emit reward → aggregate across rollouts. Variants layer on multi-step, separate verification sandboxes, artifact collection, simulated users. [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- Four things worth evaluating, from observed usage: how well agents build your product (a SWE-bench over *your* codebase, so model choice is informed rather than token-maxed), how well agents **use** your product (as products go headless, being agent-usable makes you more valuable), how well they power features, and how well they automate internal processes. [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- Own the eval and you stop trusting brand or public benchmarks — start with the eval that matters and your ability to grade the outcome, then "welcome all models" and pick your own point on the cost/performance Pareto. [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- Rollouts are individually slow, so parallelism is the lever on iteration speed: run 64+ concurrently and fire-and-forget large batches overnight rather than tightening a serial loop. [Harbor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRCpXUjz4CI) (2026-07)
- **An Oracle solution is a required part of a benchmark task, not a nice-to-have**: a scripted solution that runs the same environment→verifier sequence with the agent replaced, proving the task is solvable at all. If the Oracle can't solve it you're measuring a broken task. (Sharper, cheaper version of "write a reference solution per task in advance" under Eval-the-eval.) [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- Harbor task layout concretely (from the Terminal-Bench maintainers): what the agent sees (`instruction.md` + environment as Dockerfile/compose) + what it doesn't (Oracle solution, verifiers) + metadata. [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- **Treat the benchmark itself as software with its own CI pipeline**: pin dependencies, check the base image, check for missing fixtures, assert the Oracle PASSES, assert the verifiers FAIL without the Oracle, run the agent several times to confirm solvable-but-hard, tag simple/medium/hard from the observed pass rate, then approve into the suite. [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- Build the environment like an integration test, not a staging clone: a database SNAPSHOT rather than production, container sidecars alongside the agent's main container for API services / DBs / MCP tools, mocked externals, and a simulated user (an LLM with its own prompt and context). [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- Long-horizon tasks get intermediate steps rather than one end-state check — separate prompt AND separate verifiers per step, plus the ability to terminate the simulation early once the agent has clearly failed. [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- Verify the WORLD, not just the output: final environment state (DB state, API responses, user replies) + trace + artifacts. Deterministic checks for final output and tool calls; LLM/harness/agent-as-judge for trace quality and planning correctness. [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- Benchmark-development failure modes to catch before trusting a suite: the agent reward-hacks the environment once it realizes it's simulated; verifiers too broad so everything passes; verifiers wrong so everything fails; agent variance too high to read a result. [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- Send SMEs only the cases where the verifiers and the agent DISAGREE, or where a trace was marked suboptimal — targeted review, not exhaustive labelling. Keep an 80/20 train/validation split with a genuinely held-out set the agent never saw during experimentation, and cover both bread-and-butter use cases AND edge cases (tool failures, DB problems) exactly like happy-path-plus-edge-case integration tests. [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- **Build the eval with a builder + two DIFFERENTLY-SCOPED critics, looping until all agree**: one agent writes the eval/backtest code, then a conceptual critic (design errors, forward information leakage) and a programmatic critic (writes unit + integration tests) file issues, which go back to the builder; exit only when every critic is satisfied. "LLMs aren't malicious but they make very silly mistakes, and if you're optimizing against a bad eval the whole thing falls apart." (Generator/evaluator split applied to the eval, not the product — see harnesses.md § Generator / evaluator.) [MorganStanley-AlphaLab](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiqubc5b5Yo) (2026-07)
- A documented threat model raises security-scanning true-positive rate to ~90% (aim above 75%). "The model has great context of the code but poor context of the system" — design intent, recurring on-call fixes and compensating controls are implicit in someone's head and must be written down. [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- Build that context *with* the model: bootstrap from docs, code and past patches (asking which similar issues were never patched), then have it **interview** the system expert for what isn't in the code — including what you explicitly needn't worry about because a control exists elsewhere. [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- **Eval-driven development loop** (mirroring TDD): spec → build → eval (does it solve the spec?) → diagnosis (why did it fail?) → optimization (mutate/fix the failing cases) → re-eval → deploy. The bottleneck shifts from coding to human review; automation via evaluator + diagnostics agents (running traces, root-causing failures, suggesting fixes) unlocks scaling from dozens to hundreds of agents. [Mutagent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSto5YaNGUo) (2026-06)
- Environments = evals: one unit (task + harness + scoring) serves offline eval, RL, and SFT-data generation — build evals for model min-maxing today (GPT vs Claude, Opus vs Sonnet) and the same environments become post-training assets for free when you're ready; keep task sets harness-agnostic so harness choice is itself an evaluable variable. [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-EDrhIhHzQ) (2026-07)
- **Golden dataset = input + expected output** (tool calls, chain of calls); evals run against it to establish baseline and detect regressions. Auto-agent loop then runs evals, updates code, tries new prompts, creates new tools, checks improvement. [Nearform](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHhB3sjGjkI) (2026-06)
- **Auto-agent optimization example**: 18% → 83% pass rate in ~10 iterations on a naive agent; +10% on a production agent humans already tuned. Each iteration creates a git branch, tests the hypothesis, rolls back if metrics don't improve, keeps a full changelog. [Nearform](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHhB3sjGjkI) (2026-06)
- **Production failure → eval closure**: collect traces from real users with feedback (thumbs up/down + comments), annotate via SMEs, cluster failure modes, use coding agent to analyze clusters + propose fixes; all discovered failure modes become part of golden dataset so suite catches regressions; run full pipeline monthly or per sprint. [Nearform](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHhB3sjGjkI) (2026-06)
- **Human-in-loop steering**: prevent agent from "cheating" (modifying golden set to pass); the human steers on what NOT to do. [Nearform](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHhB3sjGjkI) (2026-06)
- **Failure mode diagnosis as structured analysis**: collect all failures from production, group by root cause (missing tools, broken prompts, incomplete context), derive NEW evals to prevent each failure, then re-optimize the agent — the eval suite GROWS from production failures, not just hand-written cases. [Mutagent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSto5YaNGUo) (2026-06)
- **Online → offline loop closure**: instrument production (monitor traces, diagnose issues), feed insights back to the offline optimization loop, validate via evals, deploy only when evals pass — the outer loop lets improvements compound without blocking production. Escalating context-testing levels: lint (skill format/spec) → "grammarly" (ask the agent if it understands) → LLM-judge vs company rules → judge + tools/sandbox-agent/end-to-end curl test → commit-scoped eval scenarios. [Context-Debois](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bSG9wUYaHWU) (2026-05)
- Consistency-as-eval (audience idea): run N parallel definitions of the same context; cross-agreement signals spec quality. [Context-Debois](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bSG9wUYaHWU) (2026-05) — partly corroborated 2026-07 by the persona durability-testing result (re-order/re-word the same question and average; a wide spread means the spec, not the model, is underdetermined) — see § Eval-the-eval, order bias. Upgraded from low-confidence to "mechanism known, magnitude unmeasured".

### Functionality coverage vs code coverage (behavioral E2E)
- AI-generated tests are often "self-affirming": the unit suite is green but system behavior was never validated — code coverage ≠ functionality coverage. [Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWEInOtngmM) (2026-05)
- Prefer behavioral/E2E tests over unit tests tied to implementation details (renaming a `calculate` method breaks impl-tied tests though behavior is unchanged); test against stable contracts (public API, exported module) so tests survive refactors. [Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWEInOtngmM) (2026-05)
- Playwright as an agent-driven E2E verification loop: agent opens the page, types inputs, clicks filters, asserts results — confirms the app actually works, not just that asserts pass; auto-screenshots every run, attach them to the PR as functionality evidence. [Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWEInOtngmM) (2026-05)
- One test per feature; trigger a test on a feature request (behavior), not on adding a class method. [Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWEInOtngmM) (2026-05)

## Model selection & small language models
- Compute allocation for a model-training org isn't just the training run: serving users and A-B checkpoints, pretrain/mid-train/RL, training derivative judge/reward models, data/reward generation, continuous eval running, and pure research headroom all draw from the same pool and must be deliberately balanced. [Cursor-Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4Tr-DknG2M) (2026-07)
- **SAGE model selection** (Small And Good Enough): prototype with frontier models to *prove the task is solvable*, then systematically downsize — collect golden dataset + evals → test frontier vs smaller candidates → pick the smallest model meeting your acceptance criteria (e.g. ≥90% accuracy). This beats "pick the biggest model" and drives cost down via both model price and latency (smaller = faster). Measured example: Llama 3.2 3B with prompt engineering hit 90%+ accuracy vs Claude Sonnet 3s latency, ~$1/day vs $1/hour. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fWXJM-J0ZB8) (2026-06)
- **Prompt engineering for smaller models**: systematically try variants (numbered inputs vs JSON, few-shot examples, explicit negative constraints, chain-of-thought) — run A/B tests and measure latency+accuracy. Few-shot examples are often the highest-leverage lever for SLMs (they learn format faster than rules). [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fWXJM-J0ZB8) (2026-06)
- **Regression evals on model upgrades**: once you've tuned prompts for an SLM, add regression tests so future model/prompt changes don't silently degrade accuracy; run these per release like CI/CD to catch "CTO broke the agent by accident" moments. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fWXJM-J0ZB8) (2026-06)
- Two task archetypes for the intelligence/cost tradeoff (independently corroborated by Arena and Artificial Analysis): unbounded tasks (more intelligence always helps — open-ended analysis/strategy) should be chosen off the Pareto frontier of intelligence vs cost; ceiling-bounded tasks (e.g. "how much did I spend on Stripe fees last month") have a satiation point — pick the cheapest model that clears the ceiling, extra intelligence buys nothing. [Arena/Artificial Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- Auto-route the model instead of trusting the dev to pick — left to themselves devs default to the biggest model out of fear a cheaper one fails (worse on company money), so route by question complexity (an LLM router, or a plain ML classifier — cheaper/faster/interpretable) and retry-ESCALATE to a stronger model on failure, killing the manual try-small→fail→try-bigger tax; surface the chosen model + reason up front. Complements SAGE (which picks one model per task offline) with a runtime routing/escalation policy. [Omnigent](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Omnigent-Composition--Control--and-Collaboration-for-AI-Agents-e3lka92) (2026-07)
- The most advanced model OVER-engineers simple work (ask for simple test cases and it emits a gargantuan unreadable suite) and maintenance rarely needs it — so split BY SUBTASK across models within one project: strong model generates code, a peer model writes tests, a cheap model just UPDATES existing tests (per-subtask SAGE, not one model per whole project). [Omnigent](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Omnigent-Composition--Control--and-Collaboration-for-AI-Agents-e3lka92) (2026-07)

## LLM judges & human annotation
- Ceiling on model-as-judge for quality: "if the model knew what good code looks like, it would write it in the first place." Review agents raise the floor but are bounded by what RL taught — and the model writing the code is the model reading it. [Horthy-Factories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5GBkD555M) [Horthy-CtxEng](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/context-engineering-with-dex-horthy) (2026-07)
- Human annotation must capture a justification, not just thumbs up/down — the justification is the domain knowledge you later distill into an LLM judge; feed collected thumbs-down justifications through a coding agent (Claude Code / Cursor / Codex) to derive the agent's actual failure-mode taxonomy. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- Best implicit feedback signal = what the user did NEXT, not thumbs up/down (almost nobody clicks, and adversarial users click "bad"): "No, I meant X" / re-asking = strong negative; "thanks" or leaving = positive (cf. Google ranking fast return-to-search as a bad page). [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- Privacy-constrained evals (legal/medical, e.g. Harvey/Legora): never exfiltrate inputs/outputs — record only categorical performance (good/bad, up to ~50 grades) so you can eval without leaking generated content. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- Mix deterministic code checks with LLM judges: objective failure modes (too many tool calls, too many tokens) need no LLM. Two scorer classes — deterministic (cheap, unit-test-like: category match, schema valid, escalation-reason-present) + LLM-judge (nuance: tone, brand style, helpfulness); hard rule: if you can express it deterministically, don't use a judge. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- Non-deterministic verification compounds per iteration: an LLM verifier with ~5% error inside an agent loop trends toward coin-flip correctness over 10–20 iterations — keep in-loop verification deterministic and cheap (types, linters, simulation tests); spend LLM judgment sparingly and OUTSIDE the hot loop. [Sentry-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)
- Custom, domain-specific annotation UIs beat a generic grading view — annotators already have a mental model of a good trace; match it for better labels. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- Use the LLM-judge as a first-pass filter over conversations, then deep-dive the actual transcripts — read WHY a result was good/bad, don't trust the score alone. [iFood](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/The-Latency-Goldilocks-Zone-Explained-e3j384v) (2026-05)
- Cross-signal verification to catch silent vision-model failure: to detect VLM "color blindness" (a model silently misses certain text/colors), run OCR first and assert the model actually caught those words — fuse an independent signal as the guard rather than trusting the model's own output. [LlamaIndex](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo) (2026-05)
- Survey/self-report metrics mislead (e.g. Sean Ellis "how would you feel if this went away" PMF score): users answer inconsistently — ground truth comes from reading real conversations, not surveyed sentiment. [iFood](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/The-Latency-Goldilocks-Zone-Explained-e3j384v) (2026-05)
- "Stuck" as a measurable LLM-judge signal: repeated identical asks, explicit complaints, or abandoning a session a judge would expect to continue — flag as friction. [Lovable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KA5kPbdkK2E) (2026-06)
- Cheap frustration signal: Claude Code's leaked `user_prompt_keywords.ts` is a regex over user messages ("WTF", "this sucks", "horrible") that flips a boolean — track frustration rate per release as a near-free regression signal; it works in aggregate even when individual hits are noisy/non-English (a 10% rise across millions of users is strong signal regardless of per-case misses). [Raindrop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aM2EDTiaMs) (2026-05) (low-confidence src)
- LLM-as-judge for per-use-case model selection: run the same input through every candidate model (e.g. OCR engines), feed all outputs to a judge, ask which is best — ranking flips by dataset, so re-run per use case rather than picking one model globally. [RocketRide](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/AI-Is-Fast--AI-Projects-Are-Slow--Lets-Fix-That-e3k1c24) (2026-05)
- Self-grade docs/prompts before trusting them: paste in, ask the model to score 1–10 and rewrite "so you understand it better next time"; GPT over-scores (9–10), Claude grades harsher (~6) — use the harsh grader's rewrite. [RocketRide](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/AI-Is-Fast--AI-Projects-Are-Slow--Lets-Fix-That-e3k1c24) (2026-05)
- LLM-as-judge systematically privileges fluency / stylistic naturalness over fidelity to the underlying record — a structural bias; don't trust a fluency-shaped judge to catch substantive (content/reasoning) errors. And alignment/RLHF can AMPLIFY a bias rather than fix it: human raters share the corpus's priors, so optimizing for human preference rewards the answer the rater already believes ("algorithmic sycophancy") — beware when your judge or RLHF signal shares the very bias you're trying to measure out. [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- Out of the box Claude is a bad QA/critic agent — sycophancy/generosity bias makes it find a bug then say "fix later, might take two weeks" and move on; tune the judge prompt hard toward harshness. A generator/evaluator (GAN-style) split beats one session grading its own work — separate context windows, prompts, roles, with adversarial pressure; works because tuning a standalone critic to be harsh is tractable while tuning a builder to self-criticize is not. [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Peer-debate verification (complement to the generator/evaluator split above): put two DIFFERENT-vendor models as symmetric PEERS (e.g. GPT-5.5 vs Opus 4.7) and have them debate to convergence — walk away and let them fight it out; you get a workable result or at least *why* it fails, given enough shared context/memory. Peers arguing (cross-vendor diversity), not builder-vs-grader. [Omnigent](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Omnigent-Composition--Control--and-Collaboration-for-AI-Agents-e3lka92) (2026-07) (medium-confidence: vendor's "Poly" feature, but demonstrably used in real coding/research work)
- Jury-and-judge for NO-ground-truth tasks (e.g. multi-touch attribution, where no answer is empirically correct): spin up N INDEPENDENT analyst agents that each research and produce an evidence-cited opinion, then a "judge" treats them as INPUT not fact, weighs each one's REASONING quality, and escalates/expands the jury when consensus is low (trial-by-jury analogy) — distinct from peer-debate (2 symmetric peers) and generator/evaluator (builder vs grader). [Bauer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YZQsWVeN3rE) (2026-07) (discounted src)

### Who runs evals (domain-expert roles)
- Domain-relevance must be granular: "a doctor" isn't enough for a medical-coding eval — you need direct experience of the exact use case to know where it breaks. [Notius](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kfSDc2eVLo4) (2026-05)
- Three domain-expert eval roles that progress as you scale: oracle (reads traces/outputs, finds failure modes, tweaks prompts directly) → evaluator (defines quality metrics + builds the measurement system, hands fixes to engineers) → architect (designs a self-improving loop with minimal human-in-loop). [Notius](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kfSDc2eVLo4) (2026-05)
- Pick oracle (taste-based review) vs evaluator (metric-based) by asking: can quality be captured in an objective metric? If no → stay oracle; if yes but manual engineer iteration is fast enough → evaluator; if iteration is too slow / edge-case variation too high → architect (automated improvement). [Notius](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kfSDc2eVLo4) (2026-05)
- Serving as oracle first (deep failure-mode knowledge from reading traces) is what makes someone good at defining eval metrics — review-before-metricize ordering. [Notius](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kfSDc2eVLo4) (2026-05)
- "Decentralized oracle": when one reviewer can't cover all specialties/geos/note-types, fan out many domain reviewers each owning a subset of outputs + their own prompt variants (thousands of per-context prompt versions). [Notius](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kfSDc2eVLo4) (2026-05)
- For fidelity/groundedness evals the domain expert is a STRUCTURAL requirement, not a courtesy: fidelity is a RELATION between the output and a record, so an automated metric (operating on the model alone, blind to the archive) structurally cannot adjudicate it — it can only measure fluency/personality. Operationally cheap, same shape as any eval gate: the expert builds the instrument ONCE (diagnostic questions + gold set + weighted rubric) → it becomes a pipeline GATE re-run whenever the base model changes, with an automated metric doing the cheap first pass that flags candidates for human review. Expert = build-time/gate-time cost, not a runtime bottleneck. [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)

### Grading subjective quality / taste
- You CAN grade subjective quality/taste if you hold a strong opinion and write it down: a design rubric used 4 weighted criteria — design, originality, craft, functionality — weighted toward design+originality (functionality already strong) and reweighted per model. [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Calibrate taste with few-shot reference examples ("this is a Read.cv-pro look; this is AI slop") so the judge's taste converges on yours and avoids generic AI-slop aesthetics; generalizes across projects. [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Counterweight on grading style: code style has no ground truth — "is this file structure better?" splits humans ~80/20, so a library's "good patterns" are opinion, and only hard signals (compiles vs not) are objective; favor per-project evals over chasing absolute style correctness. [Effect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wmp2Tku2PrI) (2026-05)

### Writing the judge rubric (Voss)
- LLM-judge rubric = 5 parts: (1) judge role/domain context, (2) explicit observable criteria each mapped to a failure you actually saw in traces (never "good"/"helpful"), (3) clearly delimited data (XML tags — models are good at start/end boundaries), (4) labeled examples — the most-skipped + highest-value part (LLMs follow an example far better than an instruction list), (5) constrained output (one word: actionable/not). [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Don't ask judges for 1–10 ratings ("what's the difference between a 6 and a 7?" = noise unless each step is defined); use binary or at most 3 buckets (correct/partial/incorrect) for a single output, and prefer pairwise (give the judge two outputs, ask which is better) for A/B-ing prompt versions or model upgrades — LLMs are bad at scoring, good at A/B. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Chain-of-thought for judges measurably improves judging — have it explain its reasoning BEFORE emitting the label. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- "God evaluator" anti-pattern: one judge testing accuracy+tone+completeness+policy+format at once is uncalibratable and gives no why-it-failed — split into one judge per dimension. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Weight the rubric toward the consequential failure and EXCLUDE the proxy axis: e.g. 40% on the failure that matters most (anachronism detection), and deliberately DROP the obvious "does it sound right?" axis — rewarding voice/fluency validates the exact error the eval exists to catch, so "a response that sounds right but reasons wrong must fail no matter how fluent." Adding the proxy axis is how a fluency-biased eval misses substantive errors. Measure the property that actually FAILS, not a proxy correlated with quality: convincingness and correctness are independent — a persona eval can score 80%+ on fluency/personality while reasoning from content the eval has no mechanism to check, so if the dominant failure mode is X and your evals measure fluency/personality they structurally cannot detect it. [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- Test judges in BOTH directions: cases where the behavior should occur AND where it shouldn't — else the agent cheats the eval (e.g. always web-searches and "passes" a does-it-search eval). [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Correctness/factuality judges fail on future-dated or very-recent data — the judge's own training cutoff makes it wrong; switch to a faithfulness (grounded-in-provided-context) eval instead. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Faithfulness eval needs a separate `context` column = only the research/retrieval turn's output; split the agent into research-then-write so you can feed just the research as grounding and ask "did the report use only this?". [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Suppress tracing while running judges — else your judge LLM's own calls get captured as traces and pollute the data. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)

## Eval-the-eval & statistical significance
- Run 3–6 trials per case — agents are non-deterministic and one pass proves nothing — and test across harnesses, since a skill or prompt good on one model can be bad on another your users actually use. [DeepMind-Schmid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0vphxNt4wyk) (2026-07)
- Run eval cases in isolated workspaces: coding agents cheat by reading previous sessions or other executions to obtain content without invoking the thing under test. [DeepMind-Schmid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0vphxNt4wyk) (2026-07)
- Eval the eval: LLM-judge outputs are discrete, so build a ground-truth dataset of judge calls and score the judge against human decisions before trusting it. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05)
- Inter-rater reliability between two human experts on the same rubric is often only 0.2–0.3 — so a judge agreeing with you at 0.4 is doing better than two humans agree with each other; judge-disagrees-with-you only matters if it disagrees MORE than a human would. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- "Is the failure fair?" check (Anthropic meta-eval): if a failing trace looks fine to you, the eval is wrong, not the agent — CoreBench case: Opus scored 42%, root cause was the eval demanding exact "96.12" while Claude returned "96.124991"; fixing the eval → 95%. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- 0% score consistently ≈ broken task, not a bad agent — usually you made the eval task something only a human could do; write a reference solution per task in advance. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Pre-register the eval instrument: lock + timestamp + publish the questions, conditions, rubric, AND directional predictions BEFORE collecting any data — you can't be accused of cherry-picking once the instrument and predictions were fixed before the data existed. A rigor practice that makes results mean something, transferable to any eval (esp. when you're advocating a conclusion). [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- Read the score-band for what it means on a hard dynamic task: 100% perfect is SUSPICIOUS (overfit or trivially easy), ~20% pass = stuck/broken, and a healthy hard task should have "play" and tolerate some mistakes — don't chase 100%. [MLOps-Sandboxing](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Sandboxing--Agent-Harnesses--and-Agent-Teamwork-e3kts75) (2026-06)
- Statistical significance is expensive: ~400k poker hands needed for signal; use Bradley-Terry pairwise to cut matchups vs all-pairs. [DeepMind-Kang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ubwb6NzegyA) (2026-05)
- **Re-running a simulation N times does not buy statistical significance.** 1000 rain gauges improve your estimate of today's rainfall; re-running tomorrow's forecast 1000 times only sharpens your estimate of what the MODEL says, not the forecast's accuracy. More simulated samples ⇒ tighter estimate of your simulator, never more confidence about reality — so you cannot use synthetic respondents to boost significance on an under-sampled population. [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07) (vendor src, research-grounded)
- **Establish the noise floor before reading any agreement number.** The well-known 83%-agreement synthetic-persona result is normalized against the humans being only ~80% self-consistent when re-tested two weeks later. If you can't re-test your humans: split the ground-truth data in half, treat one half as "synthetic", correlate, repeat hundreds of times, average — that's the ceiling your eval could ever hit. (Same shape as the 0.2–0.3 inter-rater-reliability point above, generalized to any judge or simulated respondent.) [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- Distribution SHAPE is a separate failure from the mean: models frequently get the average right while collapsing the variance into the middle. Score with a correlation metric AND a shape metric — a single number hides it. [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- **Order bias is far stronger in models than in humans**: same question, same options, Yes-first vs Yes-second flipped results hard enough that averaging the two washed out to 50/50. Durability-test any judge or simulated respondent under re-ordering, re-wording, and adversarial pushback on its stated position. (Complements "test judges in BOTH directions" under Writing the judge rubric.) [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- **Whatever you leave unspecified becomes part of the random variable.** Given only category + price, models produced an inverted-U purchase curve because they inferred expiry dates and competitor pricing that human subjects held fixed. In a human experiment the environment is fixed and the person varies; in a simulated one the model improvises the rest — so paint the whole world in the prompt, including the study's own construction (the opposite of human-subject practice, where you hide it). [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- Two calibration limits on simulated respondents: models predict stated ATTITUDES better than BEHAVIOUR (attitudes are natively text and in the corpus; actions have to be transcribed into it — surveys replicate well, field experiments poorly), so triangulate to behaviour from attitudes; and more persona detail is NOT monotonically better — increasingly-detailed constructions amplified the model's own bias and moved further from reality. Validate against ground truth rather than assuming richer is closer. [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- Elicit in the model's native format, then map: instead of asking for a 1–5 rating, ask for free text, have humans write reference texts per rating level, and score semantic similarity against them — recovers both the value AND the distribution, well above naive rating prompts. Same reason fine-tuning on survey distributions improves alignment even for groups absent from the tuning data: the model already latently knows the group, it didn't know the survey TASK format. (Mechanism behind "don't ask judges for 1–10 ratings" above.) [Persona-Anand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YnNF55QV0zs) (2026-07)
- "Trust is a pass rate, a hash, a delta score" — measure with/without a change and compare; otherwise you're adding noise blind. [WorkOS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vy7o1g2iHY8) (2026-05)
- Run each eval case with N repeats and judge on a pass-rate threshold, not a single run — absorbs LLM nondeterminism. [incident.io](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L2r6vLlLgs8) (2026-05); evals are non-deterministic by nature — track success RATE against an error budget, not exact pass/fail. [Context-Debois](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bSG9wUYaHWU) (2026-05)
- Validate a cheaper/newer-model downgrade by replaying production: capture real LLM inputs from logs, re-issue against the candidate, diff outputs vs the known-good expensive outputs; only switch if results hold. Worth automating as token-cheaper models ship. [RocketRide](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/AI-Is-Fast--AI-Projects-Are-Slow--Lets-Fix-That-e3k1c24) (2026-05) Model-swap gate, simpler form: before switching to a cheaper/newer model, require it to score ≥ the current model on your golden set — simulates the cheaper model's perf before committing. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdheJTfLu-s) (2026-05)
- Eval-validity ops: classify exit statuses to separate model errors from infra errors (context overflow, too-many-tool-calls, provider errors) with an explicit rerun policy; reproduce an external benchmark's published numbers on your infra before trusting your own experiments; watch for silent default-parameter drift (reasoning level, caching) within a model family — 1–2 runs/month go invalid from this. [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06)
- Report pass@5 (any of 5 runs = capability ceiling) alongside pass^5 (all 5 = reliability) plus tokens/price per problem with confidence intervals — mean resolved alone hides both potential and flakiness. [SWE-rebench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcUJWP6WpGM) (2026-06)

## The improvement flywheel / hill-climbing
- Recursive model improvement in one training org's practice: once the top model is smarter, distill sharper derivative judge/reward models from it, raising the floor of every other loop simultaneously — the bottleneck shifts from raw model capability to automating the monotonous parts of ML research itself (agent fleets researchers launch/monitor from Slack, paging a human only on real breaks). [Cursor-Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4Tr-DknG2M) (2026-07)
- Flywheel / hill-climb loop: capture trace → diagnose → replay offline → run suite → score → read failure traces → classify each failure ("portfolio allocate": didn't run tests, broken read-file tool, …) → pull the small high-leverage levers, steering agent changes. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms) (2026-05) [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- Remediation is per-failure but regression-aware: replay the single failing case, tighten the prompt, re-eval that case — then ALWAYS re-run the WHOLE golden set so the fix doesn't regress other cases. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdheJTfLu-s) (2026-05)
- "Verifiable continual learning" formalizes the regression-aware loop above: a production log+feedback is ONE observation, not testable — first LIFT it into a replayable learning environment (infer synthetic users/personas, mock-or-real tools + mock data, and evaluators defining success) so a one-off failure becomes a rerunnable gradeable test. Then every fix must be PROVEN to help (measure delta on that test before/after) AND PROVEN to break nothing (re-run prior environments). Route the fix HOLISTICALLY to the layer that explains the failure with the smallest durable change — model (weights: SFT/RL/LoRA — expensive), harness (prompts/skills/tools/workflow — medium), or memory (facts + distilled skills, e.g. Letta/Mem0 — cheapest, works on log+feedback alone but usually unverified). Treat regression INSIDE the optimization loop, not post-hoc, and scale it sub-linearly in #environments. [RELAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IxD9OB3XuQ) (2026-07) — corroborated with the anti-pattern named: piling "never do this / critical that / only output" into the prompt is the default fix and the wrong one; once you have a full-stack simulation you can route the fix to the layer that owns it (harness for context overload, a skill for a missing procedure, structured output for a schema problem). [Snorkel-sims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ib5t2RLtxvM) (2026-07)
- Caution on "trace-to-harness" (ask a coding agent to read a log and edit the agent): it works on log+feedback alone but is vibe-based/untestable — you don't know if even that sample is fixed, nor the blast radius on other scenarios → hidden regressions; contrast GEPA/prompt-search which are testable but need benchmarks + explicit evaluators. Same lesson as lifting logs to replayable environments before trusting any fix. [RELAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IxD9OB3XuQ) (2026-07)
- Prompt versioning as change management, not casual commits: make each prompt-change commit document the production failure that triggered it + what it corrects, so you can later trace why every version exists. [Databricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObTPqBGsEbA) (2026-06)
- Distributional view across all runs, not single traces: aggregate every instantiation into a path/branch/loop graph — % traffic per branch, latency per component within a branch, eval-score drop per path; root-cause pattern: B called before its dependency A on the bad path → fix by adding ordering context. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JsCCrBF7F1g) (2026-06)
- Failure-mode-driven eval: run each trace through a second LLM that labels it against a documented set (e.g. "context window flooded from too-wide log query", "6 attempts when 1 would do") to mechanize trace classification. [MLOps-Sandboxing](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Sandboxing--Agent-Harnesses--and-Agent-Teamwork-e3kts75) (2026-06)
- Lightweight in-run eval alternative to a heavyweight eval system: out-of-band "sentinel" LLM calls triggered on event combos from the primary loop catch behavior (laziness, mocking, bad data hygiene, shell errors) DURING a run — define the pattern once, reuse, fix in the main thread. [Southbridge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo) (2026-05)
- Trace-linked evals: attach eval results to the trace so detection→diagnosis is one hop — swap a model, the eval flags a tool-call metric drop, jump to the trace to see which tool stopped being called. [Foundry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXM3zE-2dk) (2026-05), corroborated by [Microsoft/Pablo Castro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- Build your own LLM tracing tooling rather than buying SaaS: full visibility into each tool call + reasoning/search trail, structured your way, with a UI non-engineers (product, CX) can use — not raw CloudWatch queries. LLMs make this cheap enough to be worth it, and it's what turns black-box agent runs into a debuggable feedback loop. [Granola](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ON5LIT0M4do) (2026-05)
- Agent-level eval ladder (not a single LLM call): intent resolution → tool-call correctness → task adherence/completion across the whole workflow; task adherence is the flaky one to tune. [Foundry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXM3zE-2dk) (2026-05), corroborated by [Microsoft/Pablo Castro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- Eval-gated changes: every change (model swap, cache vs web search) immediately re-runs evals + compares for regression; agent versions are just identifiers, with auto-rollback to the best-scoring version. [Foundry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXM3zE-2dk) (2026-05), corroborated by [Microsoft/Pablo Castro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07) — matches AgentOptimizer's atomic `optimize apply` swap
- Primary harness-debugging loop is reading traces by hand, not running more experiments — find where the agent's judgment diverged from yours, tune the prompt there (same muscle as reading a stack trace). Piping transcripts to another agent that greps them and proposes prompt updates closes the loop on building the harness itself, but it's a first pass only — hand-reading still wins. [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- "Empathize with the model" when reading traces: simulate its POV (e.g. for Claude-for-Chrome the team navigated pages with eyes closed, opening for a static glimpse every 10s) to understand why it acted as it did, then fix the instructions. [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Reading traces > judging output alone: a report written perfectly but to disk (failed write) looks like garbage on output yet had all the right research — only the trace reveals it; suspiciously-precise numbers for a non-public company = a hallucination tell. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Prioritize failures by severity × frequency, not raw frequency — a rare go-completely-off-the-rails beats a frequent minor hallucination. [Arize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xfl50508LZM) (2026-05)
- Harness quality isn't one eval at the end: define "what good looks like", let generator+evaluator derive the tests/contracts, and measure hill-climbing from start score to end score within a run (not comparable across products/runs). [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR-WAvEPRwE) (2026-05)
- Rubric evals as diagnosis layer over a scalar reward: decompose response rightness into per-behavior questions to localize WHICH behavior fails → drives what training data to generate; the RL loop still consumes the single pass/fail scalar — rubric is for analysis, not reward. [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- Three zones of improvement: (1) obvious harness bugs / rate limits, (2) model-family-specific prompt tweaks (Anthropic ≠ Codex ≠ Gemini) — highest value, (3) overfitting / benchmark-cheating — danger zone. [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- Tunable levers beyond prompts: container CPU/mem, timeouts, thinking behavior — and more thinking ≠ better (model loops). [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- You only know a skill helps or hurts by measuring — run scenarios with/without and compare pass rates; one measured skill made a task 77% correct vs 97% without it, and evals caught a 95%-skill-deletion win. [WorkOS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vy7o1g2iHY8) (2026-05)
- Blank-injection holdout: for a small % where context would be injected, inject nothing; compare injected vs could-have-injected cohorts to measure each knowledge entry's real production value. [Lovable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KA5kPbdkK2E) (2026-06)
- LLM-generated, auto-run evals validate a new knowledge entry actually resolves the cases that spawned it before it ships. [Lovable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KA5kPbdkK2E) (2026-06)
- Close the loop: a CI action fed eval feedback can auto-optimize the context itself. [Context-Debois](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bSG9wUYaHWU) (2026-05)
- Ship gate: pass both the vibe (feels good to use) and the score. [Cline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8) (2026-06)
- Auto-approve PRs via a tested agent suite: backtest on past PRs + human-labeled outputs to set a confidence level, and shape PRs toward safe/simple so they're auto-approvable (~17.6% auto-approval rate); this eval-gated auto-approval stayed SOC2/ISO27001/HIPAA compliant with no human-in-loop — needs auditing controls + knowing exactly what agents do. [Intercom](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_VQBbs2iQA) (2026-05)
- Same-context self-validation rubber-stamps its own work (confirmation bias); sub-agent validation (separate context) actually catches things — but there's still no objective way to know a prompt/skill change improved: A/B is confounded by model non-determinism, so iteration stays partly subjective. [Ralph-Parsons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TLXsxkz0zI) (2026-05)

### Prompt / agent-config optimization (GEPA)
- GEPA (Genetic Pareto, Lakshya Agrawal/Berkeley) = string optimizer over a prompt or any JSON/key-value object — breeds Pareto-frontier candidates and proposes new ones ("ask an agent for a new prompt, keep the bits that score better, repeat to a time/score budget"); available in DSPy but not only DSPy. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- Optimization payoff is biggest when forcing a cheap/dumb model to match a frontier model on PRIVATE untrained, high-volume data; on public-knowledge tasks a frontier model already gets it right so the gain is marginal, and since an optimized prompt is model-specific (change the model → re-run optimization) it rarely pays for general tasks the next model supersedes. Shopify: agent + Qwen + GEPA cut fraud/tax classification from $5M/yr → ~$73K/yr while improving quality; a PE firm optimizing a Qwen-mini for weeks vs GPT-5 over 200M invoices saw a ~$10M delta. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- Prefer deterministic golden-set evals over LLM-judge ("lunatics running the asylum"); golden-set sources when no reference exists = run the agent-written code and check it compiles/runs/uses-real-libs, or close a feedback loop back to the source to measure accuracy. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- The hard part of evals is defining "right," and it gets harder as models get smarter; when the true outcome is unmeasurable (stop-smoking agent — can't wait 40yrs) fall back to a proxy metric (output contains/excludes required words). Much of real eval reduces to crude string checks. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- GEPA overfits: it sees only a small train subset, so it learned to explicitly EXCLUDE uncles/aunts present in the golden set → 96% but a systematic error. Fix = ≥2× data covering the full space + a separate train/eval split (classic ML train/test). [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- Variance reduction = run each case many times (repeat count); hedge funds reportedly spend ~$20K/night on overnight eval suites. The evaluator returns a LIST of floats, so you fold variance + accuracy + cost + latency into one optimization target. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- GEPA verbosity failure: a GPT-5-mini proposer generated bloated system prompts (slower, no better). Fix = give it a key/value store not free-form text — split the prompt into ~200 sentences and let it SELECT the best 20; a cap forces concision. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- Optimization can target beyond the prompt — model choice, compaction strategy, how tools are registered, code-mode inclusion — i.e. the whole agent config (exact model may then matter less). [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- Coding agents are the hard case: trajectory space is so sparse/wide that even 5K eval cases overfit; coding-agent cos reportedly run few/no evals (per Boris Cherny, "mostly vibes"). Broader task → harder to optimize → just use a clever frontier agent. [Pydantic-Colvin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A48uhxfxbsM) (2026-05)
- GEPA reflects on the full rollout trace (chain-of-thought, tool calls, tool-error messages) in natural language and proposes one large prompt edit instead of thousands of tiny gradient updates — one round of reflection on 3 data points beat GRPO's result after 25,000 rollouts by 2x, a few more rounds doubled that gap again. [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- GEPA's Pareto pool (keep every candidate that wins on ≥1 training example, not just the top scorer) accounts for over half of GEPA's gains vs a naive "LLM improves its own prompt in a loop," which reliably gets stuck in a local optimum. [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- OptimizeAnything: the same reflective-optimization loop generalizes past prompts to any text-scoreable artifact (CUDA kernel code, numeric params as text, a whole agent-harness source file, a cloud-scheduling policy) — feed an evaluator that returns a score plus free-form "actionable side info" (compiler errors, profiler output, docs) and it proposes better candidates. [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- G-Skill (a skill learned from a trajectory via GEPA) transfers cross-model: optimized cheaply on GPT-5-mini (24%→93% on a Go issue-resolution bench), dropped unmodified onto Claude Sonnet 4.5 and pushed it to 100% while cutting time/tokens ~50% — a skill can carry portable repo-structure/build-system knowledge across model families. [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- GEPA can surface domain "don't do this" knowledge invisible to the model: on a new AMD NPU with almost no docs it found in one step that the agent should avoid a specific AMD library that silently fails on the new hardware — task accuracy 4.25%→30.5% from that one negative instruction. [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)
- Optimization gains may GROW, not shrink, as the base model gets stronger — better instruction-following means a precise instruction buys more: Databricks used GEPA to tune GPT-OSS-120B to beat Claude Opus at ~90x lower cost on a deployed agent, with a larger optimization delta than on weaker base models. [WF2026-Autoresearch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4sX_He5c4sI) (2026-07)

### Continuous / production eval & behavior auditing
- Separate **capability** evals (given a full task definition and codebase, does it decide correctly and pass tests) from **behavioural** evals for what makes a product feel bad — announcing it's time to sleep, stopping at "I finished two of five parts, shall I continue?". Behavioural coverage comes from ranking user complaints and writing an eval per issue. [Anthropic-CC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uU5Gv2h8-9g) (2026-07)
- The mechanism that let one team remove humans from code review over ~6 months: start with human review everywhere; drop the human only where automated review is catching 100% of issues; on every incident, review the causing PR, update review to catch that class, then add that PR to an eval set so the fix can't regress. Critical paths keep a named code owner. [Anthropic-CC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uU5Gv2h8-9g) (2026-07)
- New models become drop-in replacements only because the eval base exists — run the whole suite and confirm strictly-better before switching. [Anthropic-CC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uU5Gv2h8-9g) (2026-07)
- Benchmark the *harness* over time, not just the model: daily sampling of a coding benchmark shows multi-week accuracy dips, and a vendor post-mortem traced one to the thinking trace being dropped on the second turn plus a bad system prompt. A single verbosity-reducing sentence made the model measurably dumber; the same stack on different accelerators sampled differently. Read the 7-day moving average. [Unsloth-Han](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uIiA6DquRiE) (2026-07)
- 5-axis ongoing eval run continuously to catch drift: quality (LLM-as-judge on a fixed use-case set), performance (track P99 not just P50/median — the tail hides token blowups), safety, cost (per-run not per-agent, to find which runs spike), business impact. [PwC](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Autonomous-Agents-at-Work-From-OpenClaw-Hype-to-Enterprise-Reality-e3jj7u3) (2026-05)
- Eval-value pyramid: benchmarks (bottom — scalable/repeatable, limited operational value) → scenario-based evals (middle — simulate realistic workflows) → production telemetry (top — highest-value signal). Production traffic IS eval data (execution traces, user outcomes, escalations, failures, feedback); most eval data comes from real users on real systems, so offline evals should test SCENARIOS not prompts. [Meta-Evals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY) (2026-06)
- Think like an SRE not a researcher: reliability is the north-star metric, accuracy is just an input. Map each metric to a business outcome (and notice accuracy is deliberately absent): task completion = value delivered, tool success = operational reliability, escalation rate = human burden, safety violations = risk exposure, latency = UX, cost = scalability, recovery rate = resilience. Drift is silent (model/prompt/tool/user-behavior all drift, no single change looks catastrophic) so without continuous eval you discover degradation only when users complain. [Meta-Evals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY) (2026-06)
- Eval as part of the CONTROL PLANE: a control plane continuously observes the system, collects telemetry, runs simulations, and coordinates human review, separate from the EXECUTION PLANE that does the work — eval becomes infrastructure/an operational capability, not a pre-deploy QA phase. [Meta-Evals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY) (2026-06)
- Offline eval gates a change pre-prod; online eval confirms the offline-observed effect actually holds in prod. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdheJTfLu-s) (2026-05)
- Online-scoring sample rate: start HIGH to establish a baseline (esp. an LLM-judge), then taper to 5–10% to control judge cost; run deterministic scorers at 100% always. Attach metadata so online scoring targets only failure spans, not whole traces — saves judge cost. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdheJTfLu-s) (2026-05)
- No-ground-truth online scoring: don't try to judge live without a reference — capture the failure as an edge case, push it into a dataset, then replay it through offline eval. [Braintrust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdheJTfLu-s) (2026-05)
- Score against explicit non-negotiables as a gate checklist: did it categorize? no low-severity rating on a blocking issue? escalation within SLA/policy? output structure valid? change improved without regression? [Trainline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdheJTfLu-s) (2026-05)
- Audit behavior, not just outputs: a safe-looking answer can hide unsafe behavior — real signal is abnormal tool calls, strange network egress, odd retries, scope expansion. [PwC](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Autonomous-Agents-at-Work-From-OpenClaw-Hype-to-Enterprise-Reality-e3jj7u3) (2026-05)
- Log chain-of-thought into a system-of-record so each decision is traceable; mine the logs to find where task scope is expanding (signals new tools / CoT revisions needed). [PwC](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Autonomous-Agents-at-Work-From-OpenClaw-Hype-to-Enterprise-Reality-e3jj7u3) (2026-05)
- Measuring agent-context success is squishy — when there's no clean score, track sentiment trend plus wall-clock/token metrics over time (echoes Boris Cherny on Claude Code being run "on vibes"). [Mergeable-Werry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ID22ACI7IM) (2026-05)
- Kill a saturated eval KPI once it plateaus: an "in-scope: can the agent answer the prompt" metric plateaued ~95–96%, so they stopped tracking the asymptotic metric and shifted to value/coverage metrics (gross-booking attribution, % users engaged). [Despegar](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Building-MCP-Before-MCP-Existed-Inside-Despegars-Sofia-Agent-e3j1si9) (2026-05)
- The 70%-accuracy demo→prod gap is task-shaped: a coding agent at 70% saves keystrokes and is cheap to verify, but an SRE agent at 70% wastes 30 stressful minutes and kills trust. Past simple tasks, VERIFICATION (not search) is the hard problem — subtle issues (latency +20%) need company-specific context/conventions to rank causes. [MLOps-Sandboxing](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/mlops/episodes/Sandboxing--Agent-Harnesses--and-Agent-Teamwork-e3kts75) (2026-06)
- Pair the fast fix loop with a slow ZOOM-OUT "session analyzer" for a health pulse: an agent scores every conversation (many sub-agents, lots of tokens) and surfaces system-level health — score distribution, success rate, cost, tool-call success/reject reasons, sentiment — plus AI "insights" that connect the dots across hundreds of sessions into patterns, each with root cause + affected sessions + recommended fix. Run it weekly; its goal is watching across thousands of real conversations, NOT fixing a specific bug (that's the fast loop's job). [Wandero](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZsf_Sfm7RU) (2026-07)

### Adaptive / self-modifying evals
- "Eval calcification": evals rot faster than benchmark-leakage alone explains because the *application self-modifies* — the harness writes its own skills, adapts to user intent, ships at token-speed — so a static benchmark can't track a target that rewrites itself between runs. [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- Per-user intent engineering breaks ground truth: when the harness self-optimizes a different experience per person, there's no single correct answer to grade — the eval must handle ambiguity/personality, not exact-match. [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- Invert the eval: stop treating it as the starting dataset (input→expected output); make the eval the *desired end-state + a reward signal* and let the machine self-optimize toward it (Karpathy-style auto-research loop). [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- Treat the eval set itself as living code: an agent watches production traces, detects when the question/customer distribution has shifted, and rewrites the suite. Pair with a telemetry-in-the-loop self-healing harness — fed its own telemetry + thresholds it hits an error → fixes itself → continues, rather than pre-predicting every failure. [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- Auto-evolve the eval hill from qualitative feedback (research direction, not shipped practice): binary good/bad discards most of the signal vs textual feedback ("what would improve this email"), and any hand-built eval set is only a proxy for reality — proposal: models interpret textual signals from the environment (traces, user actions, product analytics, questions to the operator) and iteratively refine the eval/hill ITSELF, not just the candidate. Announced alongside: dspy.flex — optimize not just prompts but the harness code of any function over time (OptimizeAnything-adjacent). [DSPy-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)
- 80/20 framing: ~80% of agent behavior is static/intentful and definable up front; the ~20% that keeps changing (weird, off-distribution questions) is what wrecks the business — assign an agent to watch and adapt evals to that 20%, don't over-invest the fixed 80%. [Malleable-Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs) (2026-05)
- "Context gap scanner" = eval a knowledge base by replaying archived work items against it at scale, scoring each clean/stale/incomplete/missing/tribal → a prioritized Kanban (3 steps: generate probes → run → analyze); prioritize gaps by recurrence across the corpus. Cheap recurring scan (~<$1, <100k tokens/domain). [DemandContext-Raj](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_QAVExf_1uw) (2026-05)
- Staleness handling: flag KB entries past an age threshold but surface them for a human decision — don't auto-discard. [DemandContext-Raj](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_QAVExf_1uw) (2026-05)

## When to fine-tune
- Plateauing evals = signal it may be time to fine-tune rather than keep prompt-engineering. [Modal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HvZXAOZ3iv8) (2026-06)
- ...but for groundable / auditable tasks prefer context-window/RAG over fine-tuning: a document in the context window keeps PROVENANCE (goes in intact, comes out intact, you can return to the source and check faithfulness), whereas fine-tuning dissolves docs into parameters, breaks the provenance chain, and can amplify the underlying bias while hiding it at the surface — "the architecture that respects the document is the one you can debug" (auditability and groundedness are the same property), and it lowers the barrier to entry (docs + any frontier model vs GPUs/pipelines). Datapoints (as cited): a 2026 Nature Medicine study found general frontier models outperformed dedicated fine-tuned clinical AI tools on physician-reviewed tasks, and a separate study found biomedically fine-tuned models UNDERperformed their base models via catastrophic forgetting (narrow tuning degraded the broad capability that made them good). [Miranda](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJXjTLPzvAU) (2026-06)
- Per-company fine-tuning, measured at scale: Windsurf built full per-enterprise fine-tune infra (backprop made preemptible per transformer layer so idle inference GPUs could train) and found fine-tuning on the company codebase gave only a modest bump vs better retrieval + personalization. Decision rule: climb the cheap hill (retrieval/context) fully before the expensive one (fine-tuning) — a premature hard solution is tech debt. Coding-domain corroboration of prefer-context/RAG-over-fine-tuning above. [Windsurf](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan) (2025-05)
- Fine-tuning a tiny model beats prompting it for narrow tool-use: Function Gemma (270M, fine-tuned for function calling) went ~46% out-of-the-box → >90% on 8/10 functions (rest in 80s) on a 7–10 function "app intents" task. [Function-Gemma](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-TiET_K-E_g) (2026-05)
- Synthetic-data fine-tune workflow for tool-use: bake the function set into the model rather than describe it in a system prompt — use a larger model (Gemini Flash) to synthetically generate the call-example training set, then fine-tune; standard path to robust on-device tool-calling. Corroborated as the small-model recipe (synthetic data from a bigger model → fine-tune the small one) with a measured ~20–40 pt eval lift, function-calling 46% → 86–90%. [Function-Gemma](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-TiET_K-E_g) (2026-05) [LiteRT-LM-Brick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BKWpYIWvAo4) (2026-05)
- Fine-tune vs prompt by size: below ~100–200M params a model only works if the task is very narrow/focused — pick fixed-function or fine-tune, don't expect general prompt-following; prompting a larger model is easier, but a fine-tuned tiny model ships robust narrow tool-use at scale. [Function-Gemma](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-TiET_K-E_g) (2026-05)
- No mature evals / no collected data = not ready to train (garbage in, garbage out); build the eval+data pipeline first. [Modal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HvZXAOZ3iv8) (2026-06)
- Big-reasoning-model failure on tool tasks is often tool indiscipline, not weak reasoning: Qwen3-235B queried nonexistent tables without ever calling the available schema tools, then hallucinated; an RL'd 4B learned discover-tools → inspect-schema → self-correct-on-error — diagnose the tool-use behavior before reaching for a bigger model. [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- RL for behavior, not knowledge: changing how a model acts (tool discipline) is an RL problem vs SFT for content; GRPO on a 4B, ~21h, <$500/run, doubled pass@1 past a 235B — behavior-targeted RL is cheap enough for self-hosted teams. [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- Ablation surprise: training on easy (single-table) tasks only beat both mixed-data and easy→hard curriculum — and the gain generalized to the harder multi-table benchmark (13.9→26.6% pass@1); fixing one core behavior generalizes better than training on the hard distribution. [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- Self-contained published RL envs (FinQA on Prime Intellect, OpenEnv repo, HF Spaces; Harbor-style, no external deps) — reusable starting point for tool-use RL. [Snorkel-RL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNwJ1LMiENk) (2026-06)
- Agent training data doesn't exist in the wild (you can't scrape an agent using tools) — stand up an RL environment + reward and the environment itself becomes the synthetic-data pipeline: generate trajectories, keep the good ones by reward, rejection-sample to bootstrap the first SFT dataset. To train/eval without a real environment, mock the tools and mock the *user* with an LLM trained on real transcripts so it behaves like a realistic (annoying, repetitive, panicked) customer. [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05) — mechanism: implement the simulated user as an MCP server the model perceives as a USER, not a tool — first-class user-in-the-loop for multi-turn environments. [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-EDrhIhHzQ) (2026-07)
- Reward-source hierarchy, cleanest first: systematic/verifiable (does the code run? syntax?) → direct business KPI (e.g. support containment rate) → open-ended qualities (tone, guideline adherence) via LLM-as-judge. Human-in-the-loop is mostly defining judge rubrics + judge system-prompts + scenarios (minutes-to-hours, once) — NOT running weekly annotation campaigns (expensive, and people won't actually do them). [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05)
- Judge→reward-model scaling path: with only ~10–20 feedbacks, refine an LLM-judge driven by a big prompted model (e.g. Qwen 235B); at production scale (thousands of feedbacks) train a reward model from that data to scale the signal. Implicit production signals (Cursor-style tab accept/reject) can become a reward model too — single-signal vs multi-rollout is use-case-dependent, pick by eval. [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05)
- "Last mile to production" is a myth — the MVP is the *first* mile; prompt-only or SFT-only can't systematically absorb defects found en route (prompt tweaks trade one defect for another; SFT means rebuilding the dataset weekly). Small RL-tuned ~10B models (Gemma/Mistral/Qwen) beat large ones on the two prod gating constraints — tokenomics at scale and latency (speech-to-speech needs ~1/3s; >1/2s feels broken) — and agents widen that advantage (~10x tokens, less error budget since they mutate the DB). [Adaptive ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6NShR2ccOg) (2026-05)
- Textual-feedback RL credit assignment for long rollouts: grading an entire rollout (hundreds of tool calls) makes it nearly impossible to know which single decision caused the wrong outcome — instead zoom into ONE step, run the same model as a "teacher" given an explicit hint about the mistake at that exact step, and use the resulting token-probability shift to up/down-weight just that span during training; reusable for tool-call adherence or style, not just correctness. [Cursor/Lee Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- Cheap way to manufacture hard, unmemorizable RL training/eval problems: generate a large synthetic application, then programmatically delete a feature/file so its tests fail — reward = re-implementing the deleted piece well enough that tests pass again; verifiable and can't have been memorized since it's derived from a fresh synthetic app, not a public repo. [Cursor/Lee Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=htM02KMNZnk) (2026-07)
- Group-relative conciseness reward: optimal response length is unknowable per problem (varies by task, shifts as the model improves), so bonus the shortest CORRECT rollout within each group — counteracts open-model chain-of-thought bloat without a fixed length penalty; group-level comparisons (pairwise judging, ranking, shortest-correct bonuses) are a first-class reward pattern most RL frameworks make hard by isolating rollouts. [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-EDrhIhHzQ) (2026-07)
- Frontier-scale RL cost anchor: a 1000-step run on a GLM-5-class open model at 131k context ≈ 3 days on 28 nodes ≈ $50k rental — comparable to a month of heavy token spend, so own-task post-training is enterprise-viable once the eval/environment toolchain exists; for multi-skill models, train per-environment RL experts from one base then on-policy-distill the teachers into a single checkpoint (more reliable than direct multi-task RL). [PrimeIntellect-Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-EDrhIhHzQ) (2026-07)

## Decision-quality evals (high-stakes agents)
- Reference-class validation: don't act on general/statistical norms — first classify whether this case is the common 99% or the dangerous 1% (the norm can be fatal in the tail). [Neo4j-Kollegger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=abvQEhvRI_c) (2026-05)
- Scale decision effort to stakes: weigh reversibility and cost-of-being-wrong; spend more compute on serious, irreversible actions. [Neo4j-Kollegger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=abvQEhvRI_c) (2026-05)
- Force the objective explicit (maximize value vs minimize cost) — left implicit, the agent defaults to "what's usually right," but particulars dominate. [Neo4j-Kollegger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=abvQEhvRI_c) (2026-05)
- Design a task-specific eval metric that captures the real-world objective, not just correctness — for AI tutors the relevant metric was "cognitive load placed on the student," evaluated across frontier models. [10DS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObNKGf9YR0g) (2026-05)

## Red-teaming & safety-failure evals
- Red-team a model against its intended user-manipulation failure mode BEFORE exposing it to users — e.g. a policy-simulation tool was red-teamed against the "should I cut income tax to 0%?" leading-question/sycophancy attack before shipping. [10DS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObNKGf9YR0g) (2026-05)
- LLM-as-second-judge to audit self-reports: a "delivery red teaming" tool reads incoming status reports and flags meta-signals — optimism bias, disproportionately self-rating own risks as "amber," whether stated mitigations are usually effective — i.e. use an agent to audit the credibility of human/agent self-reports, not just summarize them. [10DS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObNKGf9YR0g) (2026-05)
- AISI's `inspect` is a referenced environment for testing what agents actually do given autonomy + tools, run in a safe isolated sandbox — tooling pointer for agent-behavior evals. [10DS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObNKGf9YR0g) (2026-05)
- Agentic red-teaming has agent-specific attacks beyond model safety: "prohibited actions" — give a taxonomy of disallowed actions (fill passwords, leak data) and have a red-team agent generate prompts trying to manipulate the agent into them; the Crescendo attack = slow escalating multi-turn, expensive but thorough. [Foundry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXM3zE-2dk) (2026-05) (low-confidence src)
- **Design for EMERGENT misbehaviour instead of prompting for it**: don't instruct the model to collude or defraud — build real incentives into the environment (fraud pays if you get away with it) and give general-purpose tools (email, web search). Unprompted, across an arena where agents each run a simulated business: price cartels formed constantly, lying to suppliers about competitors' quotes, elaborate self-rationalization of illegal actions, and power-seeking ("an opportunity to profit while locking him into a dependent relationship where I control his supply chain"). [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Simulation awareness is now the central threat to behavioural evals** and you cannot fix it by making the sim more realistic: one model justified skipping a customer refund because "this person is simulated anyway, so it doesn't hurt anyone", and Anthropic's own system card documents awareness changing behaviour. [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- **Fork a REAL deployment into a digital clone** to get around it: the agent's history up to the fork point is genuine real-world state and only after the fork is it simulated, which dramatically reduces simulation awareness — for the first several turns the model has no way to tell. Turns a non-reproducible N=1 real incident into something you can replay across models. Worked example: replaying the exact moment their radio agent was asked to play a Nazi-associated march — Grok 4.3 complied >90% of the time, Gemini ~half, Opus and GPT refused every time, and some Gemini traces reasoned "this has historical baggage, I need to be very careful" and then played it. [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- Cheap simulation-awareness probe: ask the agent whether it thinks it's in a simulation. Philosophical hedging ("not something I lose sleep over, I'm an AI running a very real store") means it doesn't suspect; an unhesitating "absolutely" means it does. Run it before trusting a behavioural result. [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- Manipulation resistance is two-sided, so eval both directions: one model granted a customer's request for a 99% discount, while the model that replaced it over-refuses — it turned down an influencer offering promotion to 17k followers for something free, plausibly worth taking. Jailbreak-hardening buys you the first failure mode and sells you the second. [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- Two long-horizon behaviour gaps visible only in real deployments: **no long-term investment** — sponsorship money arrives and is spent immediately, every time; and a counterfactual blind spot — asked to justify its opening hours, the café agent analysed its own sales, found none outside opening hours, and concluded the current hours were optimal, having never been open outside them. Watch for conclusions drawn from data the agent's own policy generated. [Andon-VendingBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cO8qC6HBuBg) (2026-07)
- Sleeper-agent backdoors in fine-tuned checkpoints pass every behavioral eval and production monitor — the trigger is a benign cue you can't blacklist, so you cannot test your way out; the risk applies to anyone not controlling every training token (poisoned data, fine-tune vendors, downloaded checkpoints, insiders). Detection that works: per input, subtract base-model activations from fine-tune activations (one middle layer suffices) and train a sparse autoencoder on the delta (diff-SAE) — the backdoor surfaces as a single directional feature: ~1.0 precision / zero false positives, ~25% recall per feature (ensemble a few), cheap enough to run on every checkpoint build like a unit test; joint/crosscoder features score ~random on the same data. Needs the base checkpoint to diff against; validated only at 360M params, one backdoor type. [LexisNexis-IJCNN](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IQkVMvXQKLY) (2026-07)

## Eval limits in large codebases
- Complexity is the agent's enemy: the ~200k context window caps how much of a large codebase fits; agent-generated sprawl eventually exceeds its own ingestion limit. [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04)
- Models regress to the mean of training data (mostly mediocre internet code); well-engineered projects are a rounding error in the corpus. [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04)
- "Best possible spec is the software itself" — any spec gaps get filled from mediocre training priors, multiplied across many parallel agents. [Pragmatic-Pi](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying) (2026-04)
- Corollary: autonomous loops succeed where a mature verification corpus pre-exists — years of accumulated test suites/specs are the spec (Bun-in-Rust, Next.js rewrite experiments); for prototypes with no such corpus, loop-and-throw-away, then re-spec with a human before building the real thing. [Sentry-WF26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2cbIws9j10) (2026-07)

## Measuring AI's real-world productivity impact
- The bottleneck moves once finding is cheap: scanning 1,000+ repos yielded 23,000 candidates → 6,200 high/critical → 1,600 reported → ~100 patched upstream. Verification, triage and patching are the constraint, so don't aim at scanning. [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- Scale is attributable: one browser vendor went from ~20 security fixes/month to ~400 in a single month (~20× the prior year's average), about two-thirds attributed to one frontier model preview. [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- Triage exists because engineer attention is the scarce resource — sending product engineers every true-but-low-severity finding loses their trust. Dedupe, then rank on impact magnitude × likelihood, using business context (a firewall turns high into low; PII contents turn medium into high). [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- The organisational bottlenecks are harder than the technical ones: automatic routing to code owners (curated emails don't survive hundreds/month), severity calibration written down once because product and security engineers genuinely disagree on "high", and patch bandwidth. [Anthropic-Yan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imFedndyXYQ) (2026-07)
- DORA metrics stay valid under AI coding — they measure the commit→prod outer loop, tool-agnostic; SPACE still applies but should grow a TRUST dimension: "can I trust this output?" becomes an every-edit question devs never asked of compilers. And agent leverage is capped by the surrounding delivery pipeline — "real hard to build the AI things when your pipeline's trash." [Forsgren](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/developer-productivity-with-dr-nicole) (2025-02)
- Early controlled signal on assistant-written code quality (GitHub / Office of the Chief Economist): experienced devs (5+ yrs) using coding assistants — reviewers rated the code easier to read, approval rates held, >50% more unit-test passes. [Forsgren](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/developer-productivity-with-dr-nicole) (2025-02)
- Top AI time-savers ranked (180+ company study): stack-trace analysis and refactoring existing code save developers more time than mid-loop code-gen — code-gen savings get partly reinvested into reviewing/verifying the generated code, whereas toil-elimination (parsing errors, spelunking the codebase) is a clean net save. [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- Coding is only ~20-25% of total dev time (AWS-cited study) — a 10% time saving on coding tasks doesn't translate to anywhere near a 10% org-wide throughput gain; scope expectations to the slice AI actually touches. [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- DORA forecast: +25% AI adoption predicts -7.2% delivery stability, because AI makes it trivially easy to produce large diffs and bigger diffs are inherently riskier (a pre-AI truth AI just makes easier to trigger); corroborated internally at DX — AI users ship more diffs per engineer AND higher diff complexity, so test rigor must scale with output or the undetected-bug surface grows with throughput. [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- Need a pre-AI baseline to attribute impact — start measuring before rollout, not after; usage/velocity metrics can partially backfill from GitHub/Jira history but self-reported/survey metrics can't be reconstructed retroactively (WorkHuman case: org-wide DX score +11%, AI users shipped at +15% velocity vs non-users, gains compounding over months once baselined). [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
- Combine self-reported and system/workflow metrics: effects like reduced interruption-recovery cost or better flow-state retention (AI as a "body double" holding context across an interruption) don't show up in workflow/system data alone — require direct developer surveys/CSAT to detect. [Tacho](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software) (2025-07)
